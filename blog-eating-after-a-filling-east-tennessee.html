What Can I Eat After a Filling? A Simple Guide for the First 24 Hours

You just had a filling and your lip still feels like a balloon. Here is when you can eat, what to choose, and what to skip.

Published November 3, 2026  |  Elite Dental

"When can I eat?" is one of the first questions patients ask as they leave the chair after a filling. The good news is that for most modern fillings, the answer is "pretty soon." The real limit usually is not the filling at all. It is the numbness. Here is what to expect in the first day, which foods make it easier, and which signs mean the tooth needs a quick recheck.

The Short Version

Tooth-colored filling: it is fully hardened before you leave. You can eat once the numbness wears off.
Silver (amalgam) filling: it keeps setting for about 24 hours. Chew on the other side until tomorrow.
While you are numb: skip chewing and hot drinks so you do not bite your cheek or burn your mouth.
Call us if: your bite feels high, or pain gets worse instead of better.

It Depends on the Type of Filling

Most fillings we place today are tooth-colored composite. Composite starts out soft, and we harden it in layers with a special blue curing light. By the time we polish it and check your bite, it is as hard as it is going to get. There is no waiting period for the filling itself.

Silver fillings, also called amalgam, work differently. They harden over several hours and do not reach full strength for about a day. If you have a silver filling placed or repaired, eat soft foods and chew on the opposite side for the first 24 hours so you do not crack or dent it. Many patients today are moving the other way and choosing to replace old silver fillings with tooth-colored ones.

A temporary filling, often placed during a root canal or before a crown, is softer on purpose. Avoid sticky and very hard foods on that side until the permanent restoration is done.

The Real Reason to Wait: Numbness

Local anesthetic usually lasts two to four hours, and lower teeth often stay numb longer than upper teeth. Our article on how long you stay numb after dental work covers this in more detail. While your lip, tongue, or cheek is numb, you cannot feel what your teeth are doing. That is how people end up with a painful bitten cheek or a burned tongue the next day.

Until the feeling is back:

  • Stick to liquids or very soft foods if you are truly hungry.
  • Avoid hot coffee, tea, and soup. You will not feel it if it is too hot.
  • Do not chew gum.
  • Keep an eye on children. Kids often chew on a numb lip out of curiosity and can cause a sore that looks alarming. If that happens, our guide to cheek biting explains how it heals.

Good Foods for the First Day

Once the numbness is gone, you can usually eat normally with a composite filling. If the tooth feels a little tender, or you had a silver filling, these are easy choices:

  • Yogurt, smoothies, and applesauce
  • Scrambled eggs
  • Mashed potatoes or soft vegetables
  • Pasta, rice, and soup that has cooled to warm
  • Soft fish or shredded chicken
  • Oatmeal or soft bread
  • Bananas and other soft fruit

Foods to Skip for a Day or Two

A new filling is not fragile, but the tooth around it may be a bit irritated after the work. Give it a short break from:

  • Very hard foods like ice, hard candy, nuts, and popcorn kernels
  • Sticky foods like caramel, taffy, and chewy candy, especially with silver or temporary fillings
  • Very hot or very cold foods and drinks if the tooth is sensitive
  • Sugary snacks and soda, which feed the same bacteria that caused the cavity in the first place

Chewing ice is worth giving up for good. It cracks teeth and fillings alike, as we explain in our article on nail biting and chewing ice.

Is Some Sensitivity Normal?

Yes. It is common for a freshly filled tooth to be sensitive to cold, sweets, or biting pressure for a few days, and occasionally for a couple of weeks, especially with a deeper filling. The key is direction. Sensitivity that slowly fades is normal healing. A toothpaste for sensitive teeth can help in the meantime. Our guide to tooth sensitivity after a filling goes deeper on what is expected.

When to Call Us

Some things are not part of normal healing and are easy to fix with a quick visit:

  • Your bite feels high. If the filled tooth hits first when you close, the filling needs a small adjustment. Do not wait it out. A high spot can make the tooth sore and keep it sore. See our article on a tooth that feels high after a filling.
  • Pain is getting worse, not better, after the first few days.
  • Throbbing pain that starts on its own or keeps you up at night.
  • Lingering pain that lasts more than 30 seconds after something hot or cold.
  • A rough or sharp edge that catches your tongue, or floss that shreds on the new filling.
  • A piece breaks off. Our guide to a broken filling explains what to do until you can get in.

How We Can Help

At our Dandridge and Jefferson City offices, we check your bite carefully before you leave and tell you exactly what kind of filling you have and how to treat it for the rest of the day. If anything feels off once the numbness wears off, call us. A bite adjustment takes just a few minutes and makes a big difference. If dental visits make you nervous, ask about sedation.

Common Questions

How long after a filling can I eat?

With a tooth-colored filling, you can eat as soon as the numbness wears off, usually within two to four hours. The filling is already fully hardened when you leave. With a silver filling, wait until the numbness is gone and chew on the other side for about 24 hours.

Can I drink coffee after a filling?

Yes, but wait until the numbness is gone so you do not burn your mouth without feeling it. If the tooth is sensitive to heat for a few days, let it cool to warm first.

Why does my tooth hurt when I bite after a filling?

Mild tenderness for a few days can be normal. If the tooth hurts every time you bite, or it feels like it hits before the others, the filling may be slightly high. That is a quick adjustment, so call the office rather than waiting.

Something Not Feel Right After Your Filling?

Call either office. A quick check or bite adjustment usually takes just a few minutes.
