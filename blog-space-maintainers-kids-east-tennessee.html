Space Maintainers for Kids: Why a Lost Baby Tooth Can Need One

Baby teeth hold the place for the adult teeth coming in behind them. When one leaves too early, a small appliance can save your child from crowding and braces later.

Published October 18, 2026  |  Elite Dental

When a baby tooth has to come out early, many parents feel relieved that the problem tooth is gone. It was going to fall out anyway, right? Not quite. A baby molar is supposed to stay in place until the adult tooth underneath is ready, sometimes until age 11 or 12. Lose it years ahead of schedule, and the teeth around the gap start to move. That is where a space maintainer comes in.

The Short Version

What it is: a small, custom appliance that holds open the gap left by a baby tooth lost too early.
Why it matters: without it, nearby teeth drift into the space and can block the adult tooth from coming in.
When it is needed: most often after an early loss of a baby molar. Front teeth usually do not need one.
How long: until the permanent tooth starts to come in. Fitting it is quick and usually needs no numbing.

Why Baby Teeth Act as Placeholders

Each baby tooth sits right above the adult tooth that will replace it. As that adult tooth develops, the baby tooth guides it into the correct spot. The baby molars are especially important because they hold space for the premolars and help keep the first permanent molars, which arrive around age 6, from tipping forward.

Teeth are not fixed in place the way most people imagine. They are constantly pushed by chewing forces and by the teeth next to them. When a gap opens, the molar behind it tends to drift forward and tip into the space. Most of that space loss happens in the first several months after the tooth comes out, which is why timing matters.

What Happens if the Space Closes

If neighboring teeth slide into the gap, the adult tooth may not have room to come in. It can come in crooked, come in off to the side, or stay stuck under the gum. The result can be:

  • Crowding that leads to more complex orthodontic treatment later
  • An impacted adult tooth that may need help to come in
  • Bite problems from teeth that have tipped out of position
  • Crowded areas that are harder to brush and floss, which raises the risk of new cavities

Why Kids Lose Baby Teeth Early

The most common reason we see is decay. A cavity in a baby molar can spread quickly through the thin enamel and reach the nerve, leading to an infection or abscess that makes extraction the safest choice. Our article on cavities in baby teeth explains why those small teeth decay so fast. Other causes include a fall or sports injury, or a tooth that never formed.

When Does a Child Need One?

Not every early loss needs a space maintainer. We look at a few things before recommending one:

  • Which tooth was lost. Baby molars are the main concern. Front teeth lost early, often from a toddler fall, usually do not cause space problems.
  • Your child's age. The longer the wait until the adult tooth arrives, the more likely the space will close.
  • What the x-ray shows. If the adult tooth is already close to breaking through the gum, a maintainer may not be worth it. If it is still deep in the bone, it usually is.
  • How the bite fits together. Existing crowding or spacing changes the picture.

The best time to place a space maintainer is soon after the tooth is lost, ideally within a few weeks.

Types of Space Maintainers

Band and loop

The most common type. A thin metal band fits around the tooth next to the gap, and a wire loop reaches across the space to rest against the tooth on the other side. It is fixed in place, so there is nothing for your child to lose.

Lingual holding arch

Used when baby molars are missing on both sides of the lower jaw. Bands go on the back molars and a wire runs along the inside of the lower front teeth, holding everything in place at once.

Upper arch appliances

For the upper jaw, a wire may cross the roof of the mouth between the back molars to keep them from drifting forward.

Distal shoe

A special design used when the second baby molar is lost before the first permanent molar has come in. It guides that permanent molar into the correct position.

What to Expect at the Visit

Placing a fixed space maintainer is usually one of the easier visits a child will have. We take a quick impression or scan, then fit and cement the appliance at a follow-up visit. No drilling is involved, and most kids do not need numbing. They may feel pressure on the tooth for a day or two and notice the wire with their tongue, but most stop thinking about it within a week.

How to Care for a Space Maintainer

  • Skip sticky and chewy foods. Caramels, taffy, gummy candy, and gum can pull the band loose or bend the wire.
  • Brush carefully around the band. Plaque collects along the edges. A water flosser can help.
  • No pushing or pulling. Remind your child not to play with it using a tongue or finger.
  • Call us if it feels loose or looks bent. A loose band can trap food against the tooth, so do not wait for the next checkup.

We check the maintainer at each visit and take it off once the adult tooth starts to come in.

Common Questions

Does every lost baby tooth need a space maintainer?

No. Front baby teeth lost early rarely need one, and a molar whose permanent tooth is about to come in may not either. The decision depends on which tooth was lost, your child's age, and what the x-ray shows about the adult tooth underneath.

Does getting a space maintainer hurt?

No. Fitting a fixed space maintainer usually takes no numbing or drilling. A small metal band is fitted around a nearby tooth and cemented in place. Kids may feel some pressure and need a day or two to get used to it.

How long does a child wear a space maintainer?

Until the permanent tooth begins to come in, which can be anywhere from a few months to several years depending on the tooth. We check it at every visit and remove it when it is no longer needed.
