Do Cavities in Baby Teeth Need to Be Filled?

They fall out eventually, so does the cavity still matter? Usually yes, and which tooth it is tells you why.

Published October 1, 2026  |  Elite Dental

It is one of the most common questions parents ask after a child's exam: if baby teeth are going to fall out anyway, why fix a cavity in one? It is a fair question, and the honest answer is that it depends on which tooth, how deep the decay is, and how many years that tooth still has to do its job.

Baby teeth are not placeholders that simply wait around. They chew food, guide speech development, hold space for permanent teeth, and sit directly above developing adult teeth. When decay in a baby tooth is ignored, the consequences usually show up in those jobs rather than in the tooth itself.

The Timeline Question Comes First

The first thing we look at is how long the tooth is expected to stay in the mouth. A lower front baby tooth in a six year old may be loose within months, and a small cavity in that tooth often does not need drilling. The tooth will be gone before the decay can cause trouble.

Back baby molars are a different story. Those teeth often stay in place until a child is ten to twelve years old. A cavity in a baby molar on a five year old has years of time to grow, reach the nerve, and turn into an infection. That is the situation where a filling is clearly worth doing.

Why Decay Moves Faster in Baby Teeth

Baby teeth have thinner enamel and a proportionally larger nerve chamber than adult teeth. The distance between the outer surface and the nerve is shorter, so decay that would take a few years to become serious in an adult tooth can get there much faster in a child.

That is also why a cavity that looked small on an x-ray in the spring can be noticeably bigger by the next recall visit. Children are not usually reliable reporters of early tooth pain either, so the first symptom a parent hears about is often the one that arrives after the nerve is already involved.

The Real Question Is Not the Tooth, It Is the Child

Treating a small cavity with a simple filling is a short, routine visit. Treating the same tooth after it abscesses may mean a nerve treatment, a crown, or an extraction with a space maintainer. Early treatment is almost always the smaller experience for the child.

What Happens If a Baby Tooth Is Lost Too Early

When a back baby tooth has to be removed before the permanent tooth underneath is ready, the neighboring teeth tend to drift into the empty space. That drift can narrow the opening the permanent tooth needs, which leads to crowding, impaction, or orthodontic work that might otherwise have been avoided.

In those cases we often place a space maintainer, which is a small appliance that holds the gap open until the permanent tooth arrives. It works well, but it is one more thing to keep clean, one more appliance to check, and one more cost that a filling may have prevented.

When Watching Is a Reasonable Plan

Not every spot on a baby tooth needs a drill. There are situations where monitoring makes sense, including a very early surface spot that has not broken through enamel, decay in a tooth that is clearly about to be lost, or a child whose cavity risk has changed significantly because home habits improved.

Preventive options can also buy real time. Fluoride varnish, better brushing with help from an adult, sealants on deep grooves, and cutting back on frequent sipping of juice or sweet drinks can slow or arrest early decay. The key part of watching is that it is an active plan with follow up visits, not a decision to do nothing.

Signs a Cavity Has Gone Past the Filling Stage

Call the office if your child has a tooth that hurts without being touched, pain that wakes them at night, swelling of the gum or face, a dark or grey tooth, a bad taste, a bump near the gumline, or avoidance of chewing on one side. Those point to decay that has reached the nerve or started an infection, and waiting makes the treatment larger.

Fever with facial swelling is urgent. Dental infections in children can move quickly, and that situation needs to be seen the same day rather than at the next available routine appointment.

What Treatment Usually Looks Like

Most baby tooth cavities are restored with a tooth colored filling during a short visit. Deeper decay may need a nerve treatment and a crown to keep the tooth functional until it is ready to come out naturally. If a tooth is too damaged to save, removal plus a space maintainer protects the alignment of what comes next.

Comfort matters here as much as the dentistry. For anxious children or longer treatment plans, options like nitrous oxide or oral sedation can make the visit much easier, and keeping the first experiences calm shapes how a child feels about dental care for years.

Practical Pediatric Care in East Tennessee

At Elite Dental we treat children at both our Dandridge and Jefferson City offices, and we would rather explain the reasoning than hand parents a treatment plan with no context. If your child has a cavity and you are unsure whether it truly needs to be filled, ask which tooth it is, how long that tooth is expected to stay, and how deep the decay is. Baby teeth are temporary, but the problems that follow neglected decay are not. Fixing a small cavity now is the simplest version of this story for everyone involved.
