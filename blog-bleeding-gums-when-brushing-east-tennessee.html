Why Do My Gums Bleed When I Brush?

Bleeding gums are common, but they are not something to ignore.

Published September 14, 2026  |  Elite Dental

You finish brushing, rinse the sink, and notice a streak of pink or red. It is easy to assume you brushed too hard or caught the edge of your gum. That can happen once in a while, but gums that bleed repeatedly are often telling you that inflammation is present.

Healthy gums generally do not bleed during careful brushing or flossing. If yours do, the cause may be simple and treatable. The important step is finding out why before a small problem progresses.

The Most Common Cause Is Plaque

Plaque is a sticky film of bacteria that collects around teeth and along the gumline. When it is not removed thoroughly, the gums become irritated and inflamed. This early stage of gum disease is called gingivitis. Gums may look red or puffy, feel tender, and bleed during brushing or flossing.

Gingivitis can often be reversed with improved home care and a professional cleaning. If plaque hardens into tartar, however, a toothbrush cannot remove it. A dental hygienist must clean it away with professional instruments.

Should You Stop Brushing When Gums Bleed?

No. Stopping allows more plaque to remain and may make inflammation worse. Continue brushing twice daily with a soft-bristled toothbrush and fluoride toothpaste. Use gentle, small motions at the gumline instead of scrubbing forcefully from side to side.

Clean between the teeth once a day with floss, interdental brushes, or another tool recommended by your dental team. If you are beginning to floss consistently after a long break, mild bleeding may improve within a week or two. Bleeding that continues, becomes heavier, or occurs without brushing should be evaluated.

Do Not Blame the Floss

Floss often reveals inflammation that was already present. Gentle daily cleaning helps remove the bacteria causing the problem. Ask your dental team to demonstrate the technique if flossing feels painful or difficult.

When Bleeding May Mean Periodontal Disease

Untreated gingivitis can progress to periodontitis, a deeper infection that damages the tissues and bone supporting the teeth. Warning signs include persistent bleeding, chronic bad breath, gum recession, pus near the gumline, teeth that feel loose, or changes in the way your teeth fit together.

Periodontal disease does not always hurt. That is one reason routine examinations and gum measurements matter. Your dental team can check for pockets around the teeth, review X-rays for bone loss, and recommend the appropriate level of cleaning. Earlier treatment usually means simpler treatment and a better chance of protecting your natural teeth.

Other Reasons Gums Can Bleed

Brushing with excessive pressure can cause temporary bleeding, especially when using a medium or hard toothbrush. Hormonal changes during pregnancy can also make gums more sensitive to plaque. Some medicines affect bleeding or dry the mouth, while certain vitamin deficiencies and medical conditions may contribute.

Do not stop a prescribed medicine because your gums bleed. Tell your dentist what you take, including supplements and over-the-counter products. Your dentist can help determine whether the bleeding looks like local irritation, gum disease, or something that should also be discussed with your physician.

When to Schedule a Dental Visit

Make an appointment if bleeding lasts longer than two weeks despite gentle daily cleaning, or if you also notice swelling, pain, recession, bad breath, or loose teeth. Contact a healthcare professional promptly for bleeding that is heavy, difficult to stop, or accompanied by unexplained bruising or other symptoms.

Common Questions About Bleeding Gums

Is it normal for gums to bleed when brushing?

Repeated bleeding is not considered healthy. It often signals inflammation and deserves attention if it does not resolve quickly.

Can flossing cause bleeding gums?

Flossing may trigger bleeding where gums are already inflamed. Use a gentle technique and continue daily unless your dentist advises otherwise.

Can bleeding gums be a sign of gum disease?

Yes. Bleeding is a common early sign of gingivitis and can also occur with advanced periodontal disease.

Will mouthwash fix bleeding gums?

Mouthwash may support a good routine, but it cannot remove hardened tartar or replace brushing, interdental cleaning, and professional care.
