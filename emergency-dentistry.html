When to Call the Dentist

Good Reasons to Request an Urgent Dental Evaluation

Not every dental problem needs an emergency room, and not every painful tooth needs same-day treatment. A quick call helps us sort out what is urgent, what can be scheduled normally, and which office is the best fit.

Pain

Toothache or Pressure

Lingering, throbbing, or bite-related pain may point to decay, a cracked tooth, nerve inflammation, or infection. We can evaluate the tooth and discuss options such as a filling, crown, root canal, or extraction.

Crack

Broken or Cracked Tooth

A tooth that chips, cracks, or breaks while eating should be checked before the damage spreads. Depending on the tooth, treatment may involve bonding, a crown, root canal therapy, or replacement planning.

Swell

Swelling or Possible Infection

Swelling near a tooth, a gum bump, bad taste, fever, or worsening pain can be signs of infection. Call promptly so we can advise whether dental evaluation or medical care is the safest next step.

Crown

Lost Crown or Filling

A loose or missing crown/filling can expose sensitive tooth structure. We can determine whether it can be re-cemented, repaired, or needs a new restoration.

Implant

Implant or Surgery Concern

Call if you notice implant pain, swelling, a loose restoration, or a concern after recent dental surgery. These problems deserve attention before they become more complicated.

Trauma

Knocked-Out or Injured Tooth

Knocked-out permanent teeth are time-sensitive. Keep the tooth moist, handle it by the crown, and call immediately so we can direct you based on timing and severity.

When to seek medical emergency care first

If you have difficulty breathing, trouble swallowing, severe facial swelling, uncontrolled bleeding, major facial trauma, or symptoms that feel life-threatening, call 911 or go to the emergency room. Once the medical danger is addressed, Elite Dental can help with dental follow-up.

How We Triage

Call First So We Can Route You Correctly

Calling first prevents unnecessary waiting, helps us choose the right office, and lets our team tell you whether the problem needs dental treatment, routine scheduling, or medical care first.

01

Tell Us What Is Happening

Call Dandridge at (865) 397-5422 or Jefferson City at (865) 475-8331. We will ask about pain level, swelling, fever, trauma, bleeding, which tooth is involved, and whether you are an existing patient.

02

We Match the Visit to the Problem

Some patients need an urgent exam. Others need a planned consult for a crown, root canal, extraction, implant, or sedation visit. The goal is not just to quiet pain for a day - it is to give you a clear path to definitive treatment.

03

Use Safe Short-Term Comfort Steps

Over-the-counter ibuprofen or acetaminophen may help if you can take them safely. A cold pack outside the cheek can help swelling. Do not place aspirin directly on the tooth or gums.

04

Follow Medical Red Flags

Severe facial swelling, breathing or swallowing problems, uncontrolled bleeding, or major trauma should be handled by emergency medical care first. Call us afterward for the dental side of the problem.
