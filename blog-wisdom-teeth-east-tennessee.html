Wisdom Teeth Pain in East Tennessee: When to See a Dentist

Back-of-the-mouth pain is common, but it should not be ignored.

Published August 19, 2026 | Elite Dental

Wisdom teeth are the last permanent teeth to develop, usually appearing between the late teens and mid-twenties. Some grow into a healthy position and cause no trouble. Others remain trapped under the gums, emerge at an angle, or create a difficult-to-clean pocket behind the second molar. When that happens, pain may be only the first warning sign.

If you feel pressure behind your last tooth, notice swollen gum tissue, or have discomfort when opening your mouth, a dental exam can identify the cause. An X-ray shows the tooth's position, nearby bone, roots, and relationship to surrounding structures. That information helps determine whether monitoring or removal is the better choice.

Common Signs of a Wisdom Tooth Problem

Pain or pressureDiscomfort may spread toward the ear, jaw, or neighboring teeth.
Swollen gum tissueA tender flap can trap food and bacteria around a partially erupted tooth.
Bad taste or breathThese can develop when infection or debris collects in a hard-to-clean area.
Jaw stiffnessInflammation may make chewing or opening wide uncomfortable.

Wisdom teeth can also create problems without obvious pain. Decay may form on the wisdom tooth or on the back surface of the tooth in front of it. A cyst can occasionally develop around an impacted tooth. Regular dental exams are useful because they can reveal these changes before symptoms become severe.

Does Every Wisdom Tooth Need Removal?

No. A wisdom tooth that is fully erupted, healthy, functional, and easy to clean may not need treatment. Your dentist can monitor it during routine exams and compare X-rays over time. Removal becomes more likely when there is repeated infection, decay that cannot be predictably restored, damage to a neighboring tooth, gum disease, a cyst, or an angle that makes future problems likely.

Timing matters. Younger patients often have less developed roots and may heal more quickly, but age alone does not decide treatment. Your symptoms, health history, tooth position, and expected benefit all matter. A personalized evaluation is more useful than a blanket rule.

When to Seek Urgent Care

Call promptly for increasing facial swelling, fever, pus, severe pain, or difficulty opening your mouth. Trouble swallowing or breathing requires immediate emergency care. These signs can mean an infection is spreading beyond the gum around the tooth.

What to Expect If Removal Is Recommended

Your dentist will review the X-ray, explain whether the tooth is erupted or impacted, and discuss anesthesia or sedation options appropriate for your case. Some extractions are straightforward. Others require a small opening in the gum or sectioning the tooth into smaller pieces. Complex positions may be referred to an oral surgeon.

After removal, swelling and soreness usually peak during the first few days and then improve. Follow the instructions you receive, use medications only as directed, choose soft foods, and keep the area clean without disturbing the clot. Avoid smoking, vigorous rinsing, and drinking through a straw early in healing because those activities can raise the risk of dry socket.

What You Can Do Before Your Appointment

Gently clean the area with a soft toothbrush and rinse with warm salt water. An over-the-counter pain reliever may help if it is safe with your medical conditions and medications. Do not place aspirin directly on the gum, and do not use leftover antibiotics. Antibiotics do not correct the tooth's position and should only be prescribed when clinically appropriate.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can wisdom tooth pain go away by itself?

Inflammation can settle temporarily, but symptoms may return if a gum flap, trapped tooth, or decay remains. An exam can reveal whether the cause still needs treatment.

Can wisdom teeth make other teeth crowded?

Teeth can shift over time for several reasons. Wisdom teeth alone are not always the cause, so crowding should be evaluated rather than assumed.

How soon should I call about swelling?

Call the same day if swelling is increasing or occurs with fever, drainage, jaw stiffness, or significant pain. Seek emergency help for trouble breathing or swallowing.
