Nail Biting, Chewing Ice, and the Everyday Habits That Damage Teeth

Small habits can create real dental problems over time, especially when enamel, fillings, crowns, or jaw joints are already under stress.

Published July 26, 2026  |  Elite Dental Smiles

Nail biting, chewing ice, opening packages with your teeth, and chewing on pens may seem harmless because they happen in quick moments. The problem is repetition. Teeth are built to chew food, not hard ice, plastic caps, fingernails, fishing line, tags, or bottle seals. When those forces hit the same edges day after day, enamel can chip, fillings can crack, and jaw muscles can stay irritated.

At Elite Dental Smiles, we see this pattern often in patients from Dandridge, Jefferson City, and nearby East Tennessee communities. A patient may come in because a front tooth looks shorter, a filling broke, a crown feels rough, or the jaw feels sore in the morning. Sometimes the cause is a single accident. Other times, the real cause is a habit that quietly wore the tooth down before it finally gave way.

Why Hard Habits Are Rough on Enamel

Enamel is strong, but it is not flexible. Ice, fingernails, and non-food objects can create tiny stress lines in enamel. Those lines may not hurt at first. Over time, they can turn into visible chips, rough edges, worn biting surfaces, or cracks that make a tooth sensitive when you bite.

Dental work can be affected too. Fillings, bonding, veneers, crowns, and implant crowns are designed to handle normal chewing. They are not designed to be tools. Biting nails can chip bonding on front teeth. Chewing ice can fracture a filling or split a tooth that already has a large restoration. Using teeth to open a package can pull at crowns or stress teeth in the wrong direction.

Signs a Habit Is Starting to Cause Damage

Watch for small changes. A front tooth edge that feels sharp, a filling that catches floss, a crown that suddenly feels different, or a rough spot you keep touching with your tongue all deserve a look. Sensitivity to cold, pain when biting, or a tooth that feels slightly loose can mean the problem is more than cosmetic.

Jaw symptoms matter too. Nail biting and chewing on objects often happen when people are stressed or concentrating. That same tension can come with clenching. If you notice jaw soreness, headaches near the temples, popping, or tired facial muscles, the teeth may not be the only area taking extra force.

The Biggest Red Flag

If one tooth hurts when you bite and then releases, call for an exam. That pattern can point to a crack. Cracked teeth are much easier to manage when they are found early, before the crack reaches deeper tooth structure.

How to Break the Cycle

The first step is noticing when the habit happens. Many people bite nails while driving, watching TV, working at a computer, or feeling anxious. Chewing ice may be tied to a drink routine. Pen chewing often happens during focus. Once you know the trigger, make the replacement easy. Keep sugar-free gum nearby, use a straw instead of crunching ice, trim nails short, or keep a small hand object at your desk.

If the habit is connected to stress or clenching, a custom night guard may help protect teeth during sleep, but it does not replace daytime awareness. For athletes, a sports mouthguard is different from a night guard and should be fitted for the activity. For patients with veneers, bonding, crowns, or implants, breaking these habits is one of the best ways to protect the investment.

What Treatment Might Involve

Treatment depends on what is damaged. A small chip may be smoothed or repaired with bonding. A broken filling may need replacement. A cracked tooth may need a crown to hold the tooth together. If a crack reaches the nerve, root canal treatment or another option may be needed. The right answer comes from an exam, bite evaluation, and X-rays when appropriate.

We also look for why the tooth failed. If there is grinding, an uneven bite, dry mouth, old dental work, or a high spot on a filling or crown, repairing the chip without addressing the force may lead to another break later.

Do Not Ignore a Small Chip

A tiny chip may feel like a minor annoyance, but it can expose rough enamel, collect plaque, or create a weak edge that keeps breaking. A quick visit can often prevent a bigger repair. It also gives us a chance to spot cracks or bite problems before they become painful.

If you have a chewing habit and you are starting to see wear, chips, sensitivity, or jaw soreness, it is worth getting checked. The goal is not to lecture you about a habit. The goal is to protect the teeth you have, repair problems while they are small, and give you a plan that actually fits real life.
