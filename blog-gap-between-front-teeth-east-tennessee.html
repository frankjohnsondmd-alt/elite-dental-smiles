Gap Between Your Front Teeth? Your Options Explained

Some people love the gap in their smile. Others have wanted it gone for years. Here is what causes it, what your options are, and when a gap is worth a closer look.

Published October 14, 2026  |  Elite Dental

A space between the two upper front teeth is one of the most common things people ask us about. Dentists call it a diastema, and it is completely normal. Plenty of people keep theirs on purpose and wear it well. Others have spent years smiling with their lips closed because of it.

If you are in the second group, the good news is that closing a gap is usually simpler, faster, and more affordable than people expect. Here is how we walk patients in Dandridge, Jefferson City, and across East Tennessee through the decision.

What Causes a Gap Between the Front Teeth

Gaps rarely happen for just one reason. The most common causes include:

  • Tooth size versus jaw size. If the teeth are a little small for the jaw, spaces show up, often between the front teeth first.
  • A large frenum. The frenum is the small band of tissue connecting your upper lip to your gums. When it is thick or attaches low, it can hold the front teeth apart.
  • Missing or narrow side teeth. Small or peg-shaped lateral incisors leave extra room, and the front teeth drift to fill it.
  • Habits. Long-term thumb sucking in childhood or pushing the tongue against the front teeth when swallowing can slowly push teeth forward and apart.
  • Gum disease. In adults, bone loss from periodontal disease can let teeth loosen and fan out, opening gaps that were never there before.

In children, a gap between the front teeth is often temporary. Many kids have a noticeable space while their permanent teeth are still coming in, and it frequently narrows on its own as the canine teeth erupt. We usually watch these gaps rather than treat them early.

When a Gap Is More Than Cosmetic

If you are an adult and a gap is new or getting wider, do not just treat it as a cosmetic issue. A spreading gap can be an early sign of gum disease and bone loss, a bite problem putting pressure on the front teeth, or teeth shifting after a back tooth was lost. Those problems need to be addressed first. Closing the gap without fixing the cause usually means it comes right back.

Your Options for Closing a Gap

The right choice depends on how big the gap is, whether your teeth are otherwise healthy and straight, and how long you want the result to last. Here are the three most common approaches.

1. Dental Bonding

For a small to moderate gap, bonding is often the quickest fix. We apply tooth-colored resin to the sides of the front teeth, shape it, and polish it to match your natural enamel. There is usually no drilling and no numbing, and it is often done in a single visit.

Bonding is one of the most affordable cosmetic options. The tradeoff is durability. Resin can chip and pick up stains over time, especially for coffee and tea drinkers, and it typically needs touch-ups or replacement every several years. Learn more about dental bonding.

2. Clear Aligners

If your gap is part of a bigger picture, such as spacing throughout the mouth, crowding elsewhere, or a bite issue, moving the teeth themselves is usually the best answer. Clear aligners gradually close the space using a series of removable, nearly invisible trays.

Aligners keep your natural tooth structure intact, and they can fix several problems at once. They do take time, often several months depending on the case, and you will need to wear a retainer afterward. Without a retainer, gaps are very likely to reopen. Read more about clear aligners.

3. Porcelain Veneers

Veneers are thin porcelain shells bonded to the front of the teeth. They can close a gap while also changing the shape, length, and color of the teeth, which makes them a good fit when you want a full smile makeover rather than just closing one space.

Veneers resist staining and can last many years with good care. They do require removing a thin layer of enamel, so the decision is permanent, and they cost more than bonding. For a large gap, veneers alone can make the front teeth look too wide, so we sometimes pair them with aligners first. See our veneers page for details.

Which Option Is Right for You?

As a general guide:

  • Small gap, otherwise happy with your teeth: bonding is often the simplest choice.
  • Gap plus crooked, crowded, or spaced teeth elsewhere: clear aligners usually make the most sense.
  • Gap plus worn, chipped, stained, or oddly shaped teeth: veneers may give you the result you are really after.
  • Gap that is new or growing: start with an exam and gum evaluation before any cosmetic work.

If a large frenum is holding the teeth apart, closing the gap may be paired with a minor procedure to release that tissue so the space stays closed. We will tell you if that applies to you.

What to Expect at Your Visit

We start with an exam and X-rays to check the health of your gums and bone, then look at your bite and how your teeth fit together. From there we talk through what you want your smile to look like and which options fit your teeth, your timeline, and your budget. You will leave knowing exactly what each choice involves before you commit to anything.

Common Questions

Is a gap between the front teeth a problem?

Not always. Many gaps are purely cosmetic and harmless. A gap that is new or getting wider in an adult, though, can signal gum disease or bite changes and should be checked.

What is the fastest way to close a gap between front teeth?

Dental bonding is usually the fastest. A small gap can often be closed in a single visit with no drilling or numbing, using tooth-colored resin shaped to the teeth.

Will a gap come back after it is closed?

It can. Teeth that were moved with aligners need a retainer to stay put, and if a large frenum or tongue habit caused the gap, that may need to be addressed too.
