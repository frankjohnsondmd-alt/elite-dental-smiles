FSA and HSA Dollars: How to Use Them for Dental Care Before Year End

If you have money sitting in a flexible spending account, December is the month it can quietly disappear. Dental care is one of the most practical ways to put it to work.

Published November 16, 2026  |  Elite Dental

Every year around this time, patients in Dandridge and Jefferson City call us with the same question: "I have money left in my FSA. Can I use it at the dentist?" The answer is almost always yes. Most dental treatment that keeps your teeth healthy counts as a qualified medical expense, and that can make the end of the year a smart time to finish care you have been putting off.

Here is a plain-English guide to how these accounts work, what they cover, and how to plan so none of your money goes to waste.

The Short Version

FSA money is often use it or lose it by the end of your plan year, so check your deadline now.
HSA money rolls over and stays yours, so there is no year-end rush.
Most necessary dental care qualifies, including cleanings, fillings, crowns, root canals, implants, and dentures.
Purely cosmetic treatment like whitening usually does not qualify.

FSA vs. HSA: The Difference That Matters in December

A flexible spending account (FSA) is set up through your employer. You choose an amount each year, it comes out of your paycheck before taxes, and you spend it on eligible health costs. The catch is that FSA money generally has to be used within the plan year. Some employers offer a short grace period into the new year or let you carry over a limited amount, but many do not. If you do not use it in time, you lose it.

A health savings account (HSA) is paired with a high-deductible health plan. The money belongs to you, it rolls over from year to year, and it can even be invested. There is no deadline to spend it. Even so, many families use HSA funds for dental work because paying with pre-tax dollars is like getting a discount equal to your tax rate.

The first step is simple: log into your benefits portal or call HR and find out three things. How much is left, when is your deadline, and does your plan have a grace period or carryover?

What Dental Care Usually Qualifies

In general, treatment that prevents or treats dental disease is eligible. That includes:

  • Exams, cleanings, and dental X-rays
  • Deep cleanings and periodontal maintenance
  • Fillings, crowns, bridges, and inlays or onlays
  • Root canals and extractions, including wisdom teeth
  • Dental implants, dentures, and partials
  • Orthodontic treatment, including clear aligners when used to correct a bite or alignment problem
  • Night guards for grinding, sealants, and fluoride treatments
  • Your copays, coinsurance, and deductible from a dental insurance plan

Treatment done only to change how your smile looks, such as teeth whitening, usually does not qualify. Everyday items like toothpaste and toothbrushes typically do not either. When a treatment has both a health and a cosmetic purpose, the rules can get fuzzy, so check with your plan administrator before you count on it.

Why Year End Is a Smart Time for Dental Work

For a lot of families, the end of the year is when several benefits line up at once:

  • Your FSA balance is about to expire. Using it on treatment you need anyway is free money compared with losing it.
  • Your dental insurance maximum resets in January. If you have insurance, unused annual benefits do not roll over. Pairing your remaining insurance with your FSA can cover a large share of a crown or other treatment. Our guide to using your dental insurance benefits before they expire walks through this.
  • You may have already met your deductible. That lowers your out-of-pocket cost for anything done before the reset.

It also matters clinically. A small cavity or cracked filling that gets put off for another year rarely stays small. Treating it now is usually simpler and less expensive than treating it after it turns into a root canal or a crown.

How to Plan So You Do Not Lose Money

December schedules fill quickly, so a little planning goes a long way:

  • Call early. The last few weeks of the year are our busiest. The sooner you book, the more options you have.
  • Ask for an estimate. We can tell you what your treatment is expected to cost and what insurance is likely to pay, so you know how much FSA money it will use.
  • Know your plan's rules on timing. FSAs typically count care on the date it is provided, not the date you paid. Treatment that spans two visits, like a crown, may fall into two plan years.
  • Keep your receipts. Many FSA and HSA cards work right at checkout, but your plan may still ask for an itemized receipt. We are happy to provide one.
  • Plan next year's election. If you know a crown, implant, or orthodontic treatment is coming, you can set aside more pre-tax money for it during open enrollment.

No Dental Insurance? FSAs and HSAs Still Help

You do not need dental insurance to use these accounts. Many of our patients without coverage use FSA or HSA funds to pay for care with pre-tax dollars. If you do not have insurance, read our article on getting dental care without insurance for more ways to keep costs predictable.

Common Questions

Can I use my FSA card at Elite Dental?

In most cases, yes. FSA and HSA debit cards generally work like any other card at checkout for eligible dental care. If your plan uses reimbursement instead, we can give you an itemized receipt to submit.

Can I use FSA money for teeth whitening?

Usually not. Whitening is considered cosmetic, so most plans do not treat it as an eligible expense. Treatment that restores or protects your teeth, like fillings and crowns, generally does qualify.

What happens to my HSA money if I do not use it this year?

It rolls over and stays in your account. Unlike most FSAs, there is no year-end deadline for HSA funds, so you can save them for future dental work.

This article is general information, not tax advice. Eligibility depends on your specific plan, so confirm details with your plan administrator or tax professional.
