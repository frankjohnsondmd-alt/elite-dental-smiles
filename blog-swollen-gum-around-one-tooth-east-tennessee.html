Why Is the Gum Around One Tooth Swollen?

Localized swelling can come from simple irritation, but it can also signal an infection that needs prompt treatment.

Published September 10, 2026 | Elite Dental

When the gum around one tooth becomes red, puffy, or tender, it is natural to wonder why the rest of your mouth feels normal. Localized swelling usually means something is affecting that specific area. The cause may be as simple as food trapped between two teeth, or it may involve the tooth, its supporting gum tissue, or an infection near the root.

The appearance alone cannot reliably identify the problem. A dentist may need to examine the gum, test the tooth, evaluate the bite, measure the gum pocket, and take an X-ray. Getting the right diagnosis matters because treatment for irritated gum tissue is very different from treatment for an abscess or cracked tooth.

Common Causes of Swelling Around One Tooth

Food trapped below the gumline

Fibrous meat, popcorn hulls, seeds, and other small pieces of food can lodge between teeth. The gum may become sore and swollen within hours. Gentle flossing can remove the debris, but repeated trapping may point to an open contact, cavity, chipped filling, or tooth position that needs attention.

Localized gingivitis or gum disease

Plaque that remains around one tooth can inflame the nearby gum. If bacteria extend deeper below the gumline, a periodontal pocket can form. That pocket may bleed, feel tender, produce a bad taste, or develop a periodontal abscess. Gum disease does not always cause obvious pain, so a focused dental evaluation is important.

Tooth infection

A deep cavity, crack, or old restoration can allow bacteria to reach the nerve inside a tooth. Infection may then travel through the root and create swelling on the gum. Some patients notice a small pimple-like bump that drains and returns. Even if drainage relieves pressure and pain, the source of the infection remains.

Irritation from dental work

A rough edge, excess dental cement, poorly fitting crown, or filling that traps food can irritate the gum next to one tooth. Swelling shortly after treatment may be temporary, but persistent inflammation should be checked. Adjusting or replacing the restoration may be needed to let the tissue heal.

Cracked tooth or root

A crack can create a narrow path for bacteria. The tooth may hurt when biting or when pressure is released, but symptoms can be inconsistent. Swelling beside a single tooth, especially when paired with a deep isolated gum pocket, may lead the dentist to investigate a crack.

Signs You Should Call Promptly

Schedule an exam if swelling lasts more than a day or two, keeps returning, bleeds easily, drains fluid, causes a bad taste, or comes with tooth pain. Call urgently for fever, facial swelling, rapidly increasing pain, difficulty opening your mouth, trouble swallowing, or difficulty breathing.

What You Can Do at Home

Brush the area gently with a soft toothbrush and floss carefully to remove visible food. A warm saltwater rinse may soothe irritated tissue. Avoid poking, squeezing, or trying to drain the swelling. Do not place aspirin directly on the gum because it can cause a chemical burn.

Over-the-counter pain medicine may reduce discomfort when it is safe for you, but it does not treat the source. Antibiotics are not appropriate for every swollen gum and often cannot eliminate an infection inside a tooth. Dental treatment may still be needed even if symptoms temporarily improve.

How a Dentist Finds the Cause

At Elite Dental, we begin by asking when the swelling started and whether you have pain with chewing, temperature sensitivity, drainage, or a recent restoration. We examine the tooth and surrounding tissues, check for trapped debris and damaged dental work, and may perform cold, tapping, bite, or gum-pocket tests. An X-ray can reveal bone changes, decay, or infection that is not visible in the mouth.

Treatment depends on the diagnosis. It may involve professional cleaning, removing trapped material, repairing a filling or crown, periodontal therapy, root canal treatment, or extraction when a tooth cannot be predictably saved. Our goal is to address the cause, not just reduce the visible swelling.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my gum swollen around only one tooth?

Common causes include trapped food, plaque buildup, gum disease, an abscess, a cracked tooth, or irritation from a filling or crown.

Will it go away on its own?

Minor irritation may settle after the area is cleaned, but swelling that persists, returns, drains, or causes pain needs an exam.

Can I pop a swollen spot on my gum?

No. Squeezing it can injure tissue and does not remove the underlying cause. Let a dental professional evaluate it safely.
