Your Options

Three Ways to Replace a Full Set of Teeth

Most people who call about dentures have not heard that implants can hold a denture in place, or replace it with fixed teeth. Here is how the options compare, starting with the ones our patients are happiest with.

Most Like Real Teeth

All-on-X Fixed Teeth

A full arch of teeth attached to dental implants. It does not come out at night, needs no adhesive, and lets you bite into the foods you have been avoiding. This is the closest thing to having your own teeth back.

Learn about All-on-X →
Secure and Affordable

Implant Dentures (Snap-In)

A denture that clicks onto two or more implants. It stays put while you eat and talk, and you take it out to clean it. For many patients it is the step up that ends slipping, sore spots, and adhesive for good, at a lower cost than fixed teeth.

Learn about dental implants →
Removable Plate

Traditional Dentures

A plate that rests on the gums and is held by suction or adhesive. It replaces the look of teeth, but it can move when you chew, and the jawbone keeps shrinking underneath it. We will talk honestly about whether it fits your situation, and how implants could be added later.

Side by Side

Why Implant Support Beats a Loose Denture

A traditional denture sits on top of your gums. Implants anchor your teeth to the bone underneath, which changes how they feel every day.

Stays in place

✓ Implant support: locked on or fixed in

✗ Traditional denture: can slip, click, or lift when you talk and eat

Eat what you like

✓ Implant support: much stronger bite

✗ Traditional denture: many patients give up steak, apples, corn on the cob, and nuts

Protects your jawbone

✓ Implant support: implants help preserve bone

✗ Traditional denture: bone keeps shrinking, so the fit loosens and the face can look sunken over time

No adhesive

✓ Implant support: nothing to glue in

✗ Traditional denture: creams, pads, and re-seating through the day

Less on the roof of your mouth

✓ Implant support: often a smaller or palate-free design

✗ Traditional upper denture: covers the palate, which affects taste and comfort

Confidence

✓ Implant support: laugh, sing, and eat out without thinking about it

✗ Traditional denture: many patients feel self-conscious in public

Start Where You Are

Wherever You Are Today, There Is a Plan

"I need my teeth pulled"

If your remaining teeth are failing, extractions can be planned together with your new teeth, so you are not left with a plain denture by default. In many full arch cases, teeth can come out and implants go in with temporary teeth in the same visit, when your bone and health allow it.

"My denture is loose"

A loose denture is usually a sign the bone underneath has shrunk. Adding implants can lock a denture in place or replace it with fixed teeth. Depending on its fit and condition, your current denture may even be converted to snap onto implants.

"I'm nervous about all of it"

You are not alone. Many of our full arch patients put off care for years because of fear. Dr. Johnson holds a Tennessee sedation permit, and nitrous, oral, and IV sedation are available after an individual evaluation.
