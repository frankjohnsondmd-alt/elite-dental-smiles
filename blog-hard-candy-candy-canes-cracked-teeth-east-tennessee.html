Can Candy Canes Crack Your Teeth? Hard Candy and Holiday Tooth Damage

Every December we see a wave of chipped teeth, broken fillings, and lost crowns. Most of them start with one crunch.

Published November 23, 2026  |  Elite Dental

Candy canes, peppermint discs, butterscotch, peanut brittle, and the bowl of ribbon candy at Grandma's house are part of the season in East Tennessee. They are also some of the most common reasons people call our Dandridge and Jefferson City offices in December with a tooth that suddenly hurts.

You do not have to give up holiday treats. But knowing how hard candy damages teeth, and what to do if you feel that sudden crack, can save you a painful January.

The Short Version

Do not crunch hard candy. Let it dissolve. Biting down is what cracks teeth and fillings.
Enjoy sweets with a meal, not as an all-day snack.
Rinse with water after candy to wash away sugar.
Call us if a tooth hurts when you bite or release, or if a piece breaks off.

Why Hard Candy Is So Rough on Teeth

Hard candy causes problems in two different ways.

1. The Crunch

Your back teeth can generate a surprising amount of force. When that force lands on something rigid and brittle like a candy cane, it does not spread out evenly. It concentrates on one small point of a cusp. Healthy enamel can usually take it, but teeth with large fillings, old crowns, or tiny existing cracks often cannot. The candy wins, and a corner of the tooth breaks off.

Older silver fillings are a common weak spot. Over the years they can expand and contract and leave the surrounding tooth thin. If you have several of them, our article on replacing silver fillings explains when it makes sense to upgrade before they fail.

2. The Slow Sugar Bath

A peppermint lasts several minutes in your mouth. During that time the bacteria on your teeth are turning sugar into acid, and your enamel is under attack the whole time. Sipping and snacking all afternoon keeps that acid level high for hours. It is not just how much sugar you eat, but how long your teeth are exposed to it.

Holiday Treats That Cause the Most Trouble

  • Candy canes and peppermint sticks: Hard, brittle, and easy to bite without thinking.
  • Peanut brittle and toffee: Hard and sticky, so they crack teeth and pull out fillings and crowns.
  • Caramels and taffy: Notorious for pulling off crowns and temporary crowns.
  • Nuts in the shell and unpopped popcorn kernels: Small, hard, and a classic cause of cracked teeth.
  • Ice from holiday drinks: Chewing ice is one of the most common causes of cracked teeth we see year round.

Signs You Cracked a Tooth

A cracked tooth does not always look broken. Many cracks are invisible to the eye. Watch for:

  • A sharp, quick pain when you bite down, or when you release the bite
  • New sensitivity to cold drinks or sweets in one specific tooth
  • A rough or sharp edge your tongue keeps finding
  • A filling or crown that feels loose, or a gritty piece in your mouth
  • Gum swelling near one tooth a few days after the incident

If any of these sound familiar, our guide to cracked tooth symptoms goes into more detail.

What to Do If You Bite Down Wrong

  • Stop chewing on that side. Continuing to use a cracked tooth can turn a small crack into a split tooth.
  • Rinse with warm water and look for any broken pieces. Save a large piece if you find it.
  • Cover a sharp edge with sugar-free gum or orthodontic wax so it does not cut your tongue.
  • Use an over-the-counter pain reliever as directed if it is sore. Do not put aspirin on the gum.
  • Call the office. A chip that does not hurt can usually wait a day or two. Severe pain, swelling, or a large broken piece should be seen sooner. Our chipped tooth and dental emergency guides walk through the next steps.

The sooner a crack is treated, the more options you have. Small chips can often be smoothed or repaired with bonding. Larger breaks may need a crown to hold the tooth together. A crack left alone can reach the nerve and lead to a root canal, or split the tooth so it cannot be saved.

How to Enjoy Holiday Candy Without the Damage

  • Let hard candy dissolve. If you are a cruncher, choose softer treats like chocolate instead.
  • Eat sweets with meals. Saliva flow is higher while you eat, which helps neutralize acid.
  • Do not graze. One dessert is better than a candy dish you visit all afternoon.
  • Drink water afterward. A few sips rinse sugar off your teeth.
  • Chew sugar-free gum with xylitol after treats to boost saliva. Learn more about xylitol gum.
  • Wait about 30 minutes to brush after acidic or sugary foods so you do not scrub softened enamel.
  • Do not use your teeth to open packages. Holiday wrappers and bottle caps break plenty of teeth. See using teeth as tools.
  • Wear your night guard. Holiday stress often means more clenching, which leaves teeth more likely to crack.

A Note for Parents

Kids love candy canes, and young children tend to bite them. Break candy canes into small pieces, offer them after a meal, and have kids rinse with water when they are done. Hard candy is also a choking hazard for small children, so skip it entirely for toddlers. Our guide to candy and kids teeth has more tips that apply just as well in December.

Use Your Benefits Before the Year Ends

If you have a cracked filling or a tooth that has been bothering you, December is a good time to get it checked. Many dental plans reset on January 1, and unused benefits do not roll over. Read how to use your dental benefits before they expire.

Common Questions

Can sucking on hard candy hurt my teeth?

Yes, mostly through sugar. A hard candy dissolves slowly, so it keeps your teeth bathed in sugar and acid for a long time. One candy at a time is fine, but a steady stream of them through the day raises your cavity risk. Rinse with water afterward.

What should I do if I bite a candy cane and my tooth hurts?

Stop chewing on that side, rinse with warm water, and check for a chip or a sharp edge. If you feel a quick jolt of pain when you bite or release, the tooth may be cracked. Call your dentist within a day or two, or the same day if the pain is severe or you see swelling.

Is sugar-free hard candy safe for teeth?

Sugar-free candy is easier on your teeth because it does not feed cavity-causing bacteria, and xylitol candies may even help. It is still hard, though, so biting down on it can crack a tooth or filling just like regular candy. Let it dissolve instead of crunching it.
