Stop Using Your Teeth as Tools: What It Really Costs

Tearing open a bag of chips, ripping tape off a gift, holding a pen while you dig for your keys. It feels harmless. Your teeth were built to chew food, though, and every shortcut puts them at risk.

Published November 20, 2026  |  Elite Dental

Some of the most common emergency visits in our Dandridge and Jefferson City offices start with a sentence like "I was just trying to open something." A bottle cap, a stubborn plastic clamshell, a zip tie on a new toy. One wrong angle and a front tooth chips, a filling breaks, or a crack starts that will not show up for months.

With the holidays coming, there are more packages, more tape, more nuts in the shell, and more chances for a quick shortcut to turn into an expensive repair. Here is why using your teeth as tools is so hard on them and what to do if something breaks.

The Short Version

Teeth are made for chewing, not prying, tearing, twisting, or gripping.
Front teeth are the most at risk because they are thin and not built for sideways force.
Damage is often delayed. A tiny crack today can become a broken tooth next year.
Keep scissors handy in the kitchen, car, junk drawer, and gift wrap bin. It is the easiest fix there is.

Why Teeth Break When You Use Them as Tools

Enamel is incredibly hard, but hard is not the same as tough. Your teeth handle huge chewing forces well because that force comes straight down, spread across the back teeth, and cushioned by the ligament around each root.

Opening things works differently. A bottle cap or a plastic tag puts concentrated pressure on the edge of one or two front teeth, often at an angle. Front teeth are thin at the edges and were designed to slice food, not to act like a lever. That combination of small contact area, sideways force, and leverage is exactly what chips and cracks teeth.

Teeth that already have large fillings, old crowns, or a root canal are even more vulnerable. Less natural tooth structure means less strength to absorb a sudden twist.

The Most Common Culprits

  • Opening bottle caps or twist-off lids
  • Tearing open chip bags, candy wrappers, and condiment packets
  • Ripping tape, price tags, and plastic security ties
  • Cracking nuts in the shell, like pecans and walnuts
  • Biting off thread, fishing line, or yarn
  • Holding pens, pencils, nails, or bobby pins between your teeth
  • Pulling a stubborn cork or the tab off a can
  • Chewing on ice, which we cover in our article on nail biting and chewing ice

Around East Tennessee, we also see a lot of damage from outdoor and shop habits: holding screws while working, stripping wire, and biting fishing line on the lake. If you work with your hands, keep the right tool within reach so your teeth never become the backup.

The Damage You Can See and the Damage You Cannot

Chips

A chip is usually obvious. You feel a rough edge with your tongue or see a corner missing. Small chips are often fixed in one visit with dental bonding. Larger ones may need a veneer or crown. Our guide to a chipped tooth walks through the options.

Cracks

Cracks are the sneaky problem. A tooth can crack without any visible change and without immediate pain. Weeks or months later, you may notice a sharp twinge when you bite down or let go, or sensitivity to cold that comes and goes. Left alone, a crack can spread toward the root, and a tooth that could have been saved with a crown may need a root canal or extraction. Read more about cracked tooth symptoms.

Broken Fillings and Crowns

Fillings and crowns are strong, but they are not designed for prying. Using your teeth as a bottle opener is one of the fastest ways to pop off a crown or crack an old filling. See what to do if a crown falls off or a filling breaks.

Wear and Jaw Strain

Habits like biting thread or chewing pens can slowly wear small notches into front teeth. Clenching something between your teeth for long periods also strains the jaw joint, which can lead to soreness, clicking, and headaches over time.

Easy Habits That Protect Your Teeth

  • Stash scissors everywhere. Kitchen, car, desk, tackle box, and gift wrap bin. If they are within reach, you will use them.
  • Keep a bottle opener on your keychain or in the cooler.
  • Use a real nutcracker for holiday pecans and walnuts, or buy them shelled.
  • Put things down instead of holding them in your mouth when your hands are full.
  • Teach kids early. Children copy what they see, and a chipped permanent front tooth at age eight is a long-term repair.
  • Wear a night guard if you grind or clench. Teeth weakened by grinding break more easily during everyday shortcuts. Learn about custom night guards.

What to Do if a Tooth Breaks

  • Rinse your mouth gently with warm water.
  • Save any broken piece in milk or a small container. Sometimes it can be bonded back on.
  • Use a cold compress on your cheek if there is swelling.
  • Cover a sharp edge with sugar-free gum or dental wax to protect your tongue and cheek.
  • Avoid chewing on that side and skip very hot or cold foods.
  • Call us. Same-day care is often available for broken teeth.

If a tooth is knocked out completely, time matters a great deal. Follow the steps in our knocked-out tooth guide and get to a dentist within the hour if you can. Our emergency dentistry page has more on what we can do the same day.

Common Questions

Can opening a bottle with my teeth really crack a tooth?

Yes. Bottle caps put intense force on a small area of one or two front teeth. That is one of the most common ways we see chipped and cracked front teeth, and the damage often shows up as a small chip that grows over time.

What should I do if I chip a tooth opening something?

Save any piece you find, rinse your mouth with warm water, and call your dentist. Small chips can often be smoothed or bonded the same day. A crack that hurts when you bite or release should be checked quickly before it spreads.

Is biting thread or fishing line bad for teeth?

Yes, over time. Repeatedly pulling thread or line through the same spot can wear a small notch into the edge of a front tooth. Keep small scissors or clippers in your sewing kit and tackle box.

Chipped or Cracked a Tooth?

Call now. Small chips are often repaired in a single visit, and catching a crack early can save the tooth.
