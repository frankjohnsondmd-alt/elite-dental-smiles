Temporary Filling Fell Out? What to Do Until You Can Get In

A temporary filling falling out is common and usually easy to fix. Here is how to protect the tooth and when to call.

Published November 22, 2026  |  Elite Dental

You are eating dinner and feel something gritty in your mouth. Then your tongue finds a hole where the temporary filling used to be. It is an unsettling feeling, especially if you are partway through a root canal or waiting on a crown.

The good news: this happens often, it is easy to fix, and it rarely turns into a real emergency if you handle it right. Here is what we tell patients at our Dandridge and Jefferson City offices.

The Short Version

Do not panic. Temporaries are designed to come out easily, and sometimes they come out early.
Keep the tooth clean and chew on the other side.
Cover the opening with drugstore temporary filling material if you can.
Call within a day or two. Call the same day if you have pain, swelling, or the tooth is mid root canal and hurting.

Why Temporary Fillings Come Out

A temporary filling is meant to hold for days or a few weeks, not years. It is made from a softer material so it can be removed easily at your next visit. That same softness means it can wear down, crack, or pop out with normal chewing.

The usual culprits are sticky foods like caramel and taffy, chewy breads, hard foods like nuts and ice, chewing gum, and grinding your teeth at night. Sometimes a temporary simply washes out over time, especially if the appointment for the final restoration gets pushed back.

Why It Matters

A temporary filling does real work. It seals the tooth so bacteria and food cannot get in, protects thin walls of tooth that could break, and keeps the tooth from feeling every hot or cold sip. It also holds space, so neighboring and opposing teeth do not drift.

If you are in the middle of a root canal, the temporary matters even more. The inside of the tooth has been cleaned and disinfected. If saliva leaks in, bacteria can reinfect the canals and the treatment may need to be redone. That is why we want to see you quickly in that situation.

What to Do Right Away

  • Find the filling if you can and throw it away. It cannot be reused, but it is helpful to know it came out and was not swallowed into your airway.
  • Rinse gently with warm salt water to clear out food. A {L('/blog-salt-water-rinse-east-tennessee','salt water rinse')} is soothing and helps keep the area clean.
  • Brush the area softly with a soft toothbrush. Keeping it clean is better than avoiding it.
  • Chew on the other side until the tooth is repaired.
  • Skip very hot, cold, sticky, or hard foods for now.

How to Protect the Tooth at Home

Most drugstores carry a temporary filling kit in the dental aisle. These are inexpensive and work well for a day or two. Dry the tooth with a clean piece of gauze, press a small amount of material into the opening, and bite down gently to shape it. Remove any excess so it does not make your bite feel high.

If you do not have a kit, a small piece of sugar-free gum pressed into the hole can keep food out overnight. It is not pretty, but it helps.

A few things to avoid:

  • Do not use household glue or super glue. These products are not safe in the mouth and can damage the tooth.
  • Do not leave the tooth open for weeks because it does not hurt. A pain-free open tooth can still decay or crack.
  • Do not place aspirin directly on the gum. It can burn the tissue.

How Soon Do You Need to Be Seen?

Within a Day or Two

If the tooth feels fine, there is no swelling, and you were waiting on a routine filling or inlay or onlay, call the office and let us know. We will usually get you in within a day or two to replace the temporary or move ahead with the final restoration.

The Same Day

Call the same day if you have throbbing pain, swelling in the gum or face, a bad taste, a fever, or if a piece of the tooth broke off with the filling. These can point to infection or a cracked tooth. Our guide to a dental emergency covers what to do in the meantime, and our emergency dentistry page explains how we handle urgent visits.

If You Are Mid Root Canal

Call within 24 hours even if it does not hurt. Replacing the seal quickly protects the work that was already done. Mild soreness after a root canal is common, but sharp or increasing pain is not. See tooth pain after a root canal for what is normal.

What About a Temporary Crown?

A temporary crown coming off is a similar situation with a few differences. You can often slip it back on with a dab of drugstore dental cement or even toothpaste to hold it for a short time. Our article on temporary crown care walks through it step by step. If a permanent filling breaks instead, read about a broken filling.

How to Keep the Next Temporary in Place

  • Chew on the opposite side until your final restoration is placed.
  • Avoid gum, caramel, taffy, and other sticky foods.
  • Floss carefully: slide the floss out the side instead of snapping it back up.
  • Wear your night guard if you grind.
  • Keep your follow-up appointment. The longer a temporary stays in, the more likely it is to fail.

That last point is the big one. A temporary is a bridge to the final repair, not a substitute for it. Finishing treatment on time protects the tooth and usually saves money compared with fixing a problem that grew while you waited.

Common Questions

How long can I go without a temporary filling?

Try to be seen within a few days. A tooth without its temporary can pick up bacteria, break, or shift, and a tooth that is mid root canal can become reinfected. If you have pain or swelling, call the same day.

Can I replace a temporary filling myself?

You can cover the opening with a drugstore temporary filling material or sugar-free gum to protect it for a day or two. This is a stopgap, not a fix. Do not use household glue, and still call your dentist to have it replaced properly.

Is it an emergency if my temporary filling falls out during a root canal?

It is not usually an emergency, but it is urgent. The temporary seals the inside of the tooth. Without it, saliva and bacteria can leak in and undo the cleaning that was done. Call your dentist within a day or two, sooner if you have pain or swelling.

Lost a Temporary Filling?

Call us. We will get you in quickly to seal and protect the tooth.
