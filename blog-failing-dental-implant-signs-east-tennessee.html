Signs of a Failing Dental Implant (and What Can Be Done)

Dental implants are one of the most reliable treatments in dentistry, but they are not maintenance free. Here is how to tell when something is wrong, what is usually fixable, and when to call.

Published October 30, 2026  |  Elite Dental

Most dental implants last for decades. Long-term studies commonly report success rates around 95 percent at ten years. That still leaves a small number that run into trouble, and the earlier a problem is caught, the more options you have. The good news is that many "implant problems" turn out to be issues with the crown or the gums around the implant, not the implant itself.

The Short Version

Most common late problem: gum infection around the implant, called peri-implantitis.
Warning signs: bleeding, swelling, pus, a bad taste, gum shrinking back, or anything that feels loose.
Loose crown vs. loose implant: a loose crown is often a simple fix. A loose implant post is a bigger problem.
What to do: call promptly. Early gum inflammation around an implant is often reversible.

Early Failure vs. Late Failure

Implant problems tend to fall into two windows.

Early failure happens in the first few months, before the bone has fully bonded to the implant. This bonding process is called osseointegration. If it does not happen, the implant never becomes solid. Causes include infection during healing, smoking, too much pressure on the implant too soon, or bone that was not dense enough to begin with. Our implant healing timeline explains what normal healing looks like.

Late failure happens after the implant has been working well, sometimes for years. The usual causes are gum infection that slowly destroys the supporting bone, or heavy bite forces from clenching and grinding.

Warning Signs to Watch For

  • Bleeding when you brush or floss around the implant. Healthy tissue around an implant should not bleed regularly.
  • Red, puffy, or tender gums around the implant crown.
  • Pus or a bad taste coming from the area.
  • Gum shrinking back so the crown looks longer, or a gray metal edge starts to show.
  • Movement. Any wiggle in the crown or the implant.
  • Pain that develops later. Some soreness after surgery is normal. New pain months or years later is not.
  • A change in your bite or the crown feeling high when you chew.

Peri-Implantitis: The Most Common Late Problem

Implants cannot get cavities, but the gums and bone around them can still get infected. It starts as peri-implant mucositis, which is inflammation of the gums only. At this stage it behaves a lot like gingivitis and is usually reversible with professional cleaning and better home care.

If it is not treated, it can progress to peri-implantitis, where the infection starts eating away the bone that holds the implant. This often happens quietly, without much pain, which is why regular checkups and X-rays matter so much for implant patients. People with a history of gum disease are at higher risk, and our article on periodontal maintenance explains why those patients benefit from more frequent cleanings.

Is It the Crown or the Implant?

This is the question we sort out first, because the answer changes everything. An implant has three parts: the post in the bone, a connector called an abutment, and the crown on top. If the crown feels loose, the most common cause is a loosened abutment screw or a crown that has come uncemented. Those are usually quick, inexpensive fixes.

If the post itself moves, the bone bond has been lost. A mobile implant cannot be tightened back into place. It needs to be removed, and the site may need time to heal or a bone graft before a new implant is placed. Either way, do not wait to find out which one you have. A loose screw left alone can damage the parts or let bacteria in.

What Raises the Risk

  • Smoking and vaping, which reduce blood flow and slow healing.
  • Uncontrolled diabetes, which makes infection more likely and healing slower.
  • A history of gum disease.
  • Clenching or grinding, which overloads the implant. A custom night guard protects your investment.
  • Skipping cleanings or not cleaning around the implant at home.
  • Leftover cement under the gums from when the crown was placed, which can quietly irritate the tissue.

Can a Failing Implant Be Saved?

Often, yes, if it is caught early. Gum inflammation alone is usually reversed with a thorough cleaning, antimicrobial rinses, and better home care. Early bone loss may be treated by cleaning the implant surface and, in some cases, a minor gum procedure or bone grafting to rebuild support. When too much bone has been lost or the implant is loose, removal is the right call. That is not the end of the road. Many patients go on to have a new implant placed successfully once the area heals.

How to Keep Your Implant Healthy

Brush twice a day, clean around the implant daily with floss, a water flosser, or small interdental brushes, and keep your regular cleaning visits. Tell us if you notice bleeding or tenderness instead of waiting for your next appointment. Our guide on how to care for dental implants walks through the daily routine in more detail.

How We Can Help

At our Dandridge and Jefferson City offices, we check implants at every visit, whether we placed them or another dentist did. We look at the gums, measure around the implant, test for movement, and take X-rays when needed to see the bone. If something is off, we will tell you plainly whether it is a quick fix or something bigger, and what your options are.

Common Questions

Can a dental implant fail after 10 years?

Yes, though it is uncommon. Late failures are usually caused by gum infection around the implant or heavy grinding. Regular cleanings, X-rays, and good home care are the best protection.

Does a failing implant hurt?

Not always. Peri-implantitis can destroy bone with little or no pain, which is why bleeding, swelling, or gum recession around an implant should be checked even if it does not hurt.

My implant crown feels loose. Is the implant failing?

Not necessarily. A loose crown is most often caused by a loosened screw or failed cement, which is usually an easy repair. Call soon so we can check it before the parts or the gums are damaged.

Something Feel Off With Your Implant?

Call either office. We will check it, tell you exactly what is going on, and help you protect it for the long run.
