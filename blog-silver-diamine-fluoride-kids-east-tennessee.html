Silver Diamine Fluoride for Kids: How It Stops Cavities Without Drilling

A cavity in a three-year-old used to mean a shot and a drill, or sedation. Today there is often a gentler first step: a liquid painted on the tooth in under a minute that can stop decay in its tracks.

Published November 1, 2026  |  Elite Dental

Silver diamine fluoride, usually called SDF, has changed how many dentists handle cavities in young children. It is not a replacement for every filling, and it comes with one big tradeoff parents need to know about up front. But for the right child and the right tooth, it can turn a stressful appointment into a quick, painless one. Here is how it works, what to expect, and when it makes sense.

The Short Version

What it is: a clear liquid of silver and fluoride brushed onto a cavity.
What it does: stops active decay from spreading in most treated cavities, especially with repeat applications.
What it costs you: the decayed spot turns permanently black.
Who it fits: young or anxious children, kids with many cavities, and baby teeth that will fall out in a few years.

How Silver Diamine Fluoride Works

SDF does two jobs at once. The silver kills the bacteria living inside the cavity, and the fluoride hardens the softened tooth structure so it resists further breakdown. Together they "arrest" the decay, meaning the cavity stops growing. The hole does not fill back in, but it stops getting deeper and stops causing trouble.

The process is simple. We dry the tooth, protect the gums and lips, and paint a tiny drop of SDF onto the cavity with a micro brush. It sits for about a minute, and that is it. No needle, no drill, and no numb lip for the rest of the afternoon. Most children are done before they have time to get worried.

The Tradeoff: Black Staining

This is the part every parent should hear before saying yes. When SDF reacts with decayed tooth structure, the treated area turns dark brown or black. That stain is permanent. It is actually a sign the treatment is working, because it shows where the silver has sealed off the decay.

Healthy enamel around the cavity does not stain, only the decayed spot. On a back molar, most people will never notice. On a front tooth, it is very visible, and some families decide that is not worth it. Others are happy to trade a dark spot on a baby tooth for avoiding a shot, a drill, or sedation. There is no wrong answer. It is your call, and we will show you where the stain would be before we start.

SDF can also leave a temporary stain on skin or clothing if it drips, so we protect the area carefully. A skin stain fades over a few days to a couple of weeks.

Who Is a Good Candidate

SDF tends to work best for:

  • Toddlers and preschoolers who are too young to sit through a traditional filling.
  • Anxious children or kids with special needs, where a short, painless visit matters a lot. Our guide to dental visits for kids with sensory sensitivities covers more ways we adapt care.
  • Children with several cavities, where SDF can stabilize everything quickly while a longer-term plan is made.
  • Baby teeth that will fall out soon, where a full filling may not be worth it.
  • Kids who need time. SDF can hold decay in check until a child is older and ready for treatment, or until sedation can be scheduled.

When SDF Is Not the Right Choice

It is not for every tooth. SDF is generally not used when:

  • The cavity has already reached the nerve, or the tooth is causing ongoing pain or swelling. That usually means an infection that needs a pulpotomy or extraction.
  • Your child has a known silver allergy.
  • There are open sores or ulcers in the mouth.
  • The cavity is large enough that food packs into it or the tooth may break. In those cases a filling or crown may be added over the treated area.
  • The stain would be on a front tooth and the family prefers to avoid it.

What Happens After Treatment

SDF works best as a series, not a one-time fix. We typically reapply it at follow-up visits, often every six months, and check that the treated spots stay hard and dark. Soft, light-colored areas mean decay may still be active and need another coat or a different approach.

After the visit, your child can eat and drink normally, though we usually suggest waiting about an hour. There may be a brief metallic taste. Keep brushing twice a day with a pea-sized smear of fluoride toothpaste, and see our tips on how much toothpaste kids need.

SDF Does Not Fix the Cause

SDF stops the cavities that are already there. It does not stop new ones. If a child is getting cavities, something in the daily routine is feeding them: bedtime bottles or sippy cups of milk or juice, frequent snacking, sticky foods, or brushing that is not happening consistently. Our article on cavities in baby teeth explains why those early teeth matter so much, and sealants and fluoride varnish can help protect the teeth that are still healthy.

Common Questions

Does silver diamine fluoride hurt?

No. There is no drilling, no shot, and no numbing. The liquid is painted on with a tiny brush and left to dry for about a minute. Some children notice a metallic taste for a short time afterward.

Will the black stain go away?

No. The treated decay stays dark for good, because the stain is part of how SDF works. Healthy tooth structure around it does not turn black. On baby teeth, the stain leaves when the tooth falls out, and a tooth-colored filling can cover it later if needed.

Is silver diamine fluoride safe for young children?

Yes, when used as directed. Only a very small drop is used per visit. It is not a good fit for children with a silver allergy or with sores or ulcers in the mouth, so we review your child's health history before using it.
