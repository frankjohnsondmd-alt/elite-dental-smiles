Dental Care During Pregnancy in East Tennessee

Routine dental care is an important part of a healthy pregnancy. Here is what expectant parents should know.

Published September 22, 2026  |  Elite Dental

Pregnancy brings plenty of appointments, questions, and changes. Dental care can easily fall lower on the list, especially when patients are unsure which treatments are safe. The reassuring answer is that preventive and necessary dental care can usually continue during pregnancy. In fact, delaying care may allow a small problem to become painful or infected.

Tell your dentist that you are pregnant, how far along you are, and whether your obstetric provider has given you any special instructions. That information helps the dental team plan comfortable, appropriate care for you and your baby.

Why Gums Can Change During Pregnancy

Hormonal changes can make gum tissue react more strongly to plaque. You may notice redness, puffiness, tenderness, or bleeding when brushing and flossing. This is often called pregnancy gingivitis. It is common, but it should not be ignored. Professional cleanings and careful daily home care help control the bacteria that drive inflammation.

Brush twice daily with fluoride toothpaste and clean between your teeth once a day. If nausea makes brushing difficult, try a smaller toothbrush, a mild-flavored toothpaste, or brushing at a different time. Consistency matters more than forcing a routine that triggers your gag reflex.

Are Dental Cleanings and Treatment Safe?

Routine exams and cleanings are generally safe during pregnancy. Necessary fillings, root canal treatment, and extractions can also be performed when needed. The second trimester is often the most comfortable time for planned treatment, but urgent problems should be evaluated during any trimester.

Local anesthetics commonly used in dentistry have established uses during pregnancy. Your dentist will review your health history and select appropriate medications. Never stop a prescribed medication or take a new pain reliever or antibiotic without guidance from the clinician managing it.

Do Not Wait on Signs of Infection

Call a dentist promptly for facial swelling, fever, pus, a bad taste, severe tooth pain, difficulty swallowing, or pain that interrupts sleep. Dental infections do not resolve on their own and can become more complicated when treatment is postponed.

What About Dental X-Rays?

Dental X-rays may be recommended when they are needed to diagnose pain, infection, decay, or another condition that cannot be evaluated by sight alone. Modern digital dental X-rays use a very small amount of radiation and focus on a limited area. Your dental team will follow current safety practices and avoid images that are not clinically necessary.

If an X-ray can reasonably wait until after delivery, your dentist may postpone it. If it is needed to diagnose an urgent problem, the benefit of finding and treating the cause usually outweighs the risk of leaving the condition unknown.

Morning Sickness and Tooth Enamel

Vomiting exposes teeth to stomach acid, which can temporarily soften enamel. Do not brush immediately afterward. Rinse with water or a mixture of one teaspoon of baking soda in a cup of water, then wait about 30 minutes before brushing. Frequent snacking may also increase cavity risk, so drink water and choose lower-sugar options when possible.

Some patients develop dry mouth, while others produce more saliva than usual. Staying hydrated and chewing sugar-free gum can help dry mouth. Let your dentist know if reflux or vomiting is frequent so the team can look for early enamel changes and suggest additional protection.

Pregnancy Growths on the Gums

A small number of patients develop a red, raised gum growth that bleeds easily. These are sometimes called pregnancy tumors, but they are not cancer. They are usually an exaggerated inflammatory response to plaque or irritation. Many shrink after delivery, although a dentist should examine any new oral lump to confirm what it is and decide whether treatment is needed.

Plan Ahead for a Healthier Smile

If possible, schedule an exam and cleaning early in pregnancy. It gives your dentist a chance to address inflammation, identify problems before they become emergencies, and tailor home-care recommendations. After delivery, keep regular appointments and remember that a parent’s oral health habits help shape a child’s future habits too.

At your appointment, bring an updated medication list and the contact information for your obstetric provider. Mention high-risk pregnancy concerns, blood pressure changes, gestational diabetes, or instructions you have received about medications and procedures. As pregnancy advances, shorter appointments and a slightly adjusted chair position may feel better. If you become lightheaded, uncomfortable, or short of breath during care, tell the dental team immediately so they can reposition you and take a break.
