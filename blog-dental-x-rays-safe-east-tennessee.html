Are Dental X-rays Safe?

Why digital dental X-rays matter, when they are recommended, and what East Tennessee families should know.

Published August 3, 2026  |  Elite Dental

Dental X-rays are one of the most common questions patients ask about during a checkup. That makes sense. If your teeth feel fine, it is natural to wonder why an X-ray is needed at all, or whether it is safe to take one as part of routine dental care.

The short answer is that dental X-rays are considered safe when used appropriately, and they give your dentist information that cannot always be seen during a visual exam. At Elite Dental, X-rays are recommended based on your age, dental history, symptoms, risk level, and the type of care being planned. They are not taken just to check a box.

What Dental X-rays Can Show

A dentist can see a lot by looking directly at your teeth and gums, but some problems start between teeth, under older fillings, below the gumline, or around the roots. Dental X-rays can help find cavities between teeth, bone loss from gum disease, infections, impacted teeth, root problems, and changes around crowns, bridges, implants, and fillings.

That matters because dental pain often shows up late. A tooth can have decay under an old filling before it hurts. Gum disease can affect bone support before a tooth feels loose. An abscess can begin around a root before swelling appears. X-rays help us catch problems while they are smaller, easier to explain, and often easier to treat.

How Often Do You Need Dental X-rays?

There is no single schedule that fits every patient. Someone with a history of frequent cavities, dry mouth, gum disease, complex dental work, or new symptoms may need X-rays more often than someone with very stable oral health. Children and teens may need X-rays to monitor growth, tooth eruption, spacing, and cavity risk.

If your mouth has been healthy for years and there are no symptoms, your dentist may recommend them less often. If a tooth hurts, a crown feels loose, a filling breaks, or swelling appears, a focused X-ray may be needed right away to diagnose the issue. The goal is always to use the right image at the right time.

Digital X-rays Keep Exposure Low

Modern digital dental X-rays use a low amount of radiation and are focused on a small area. They also give the dentist immediate images, which helps with diagnosis and treatment planning.

Why X-rays Matter Even When Nothing Hurts

Waiting for pain is a poor dental strategy. By the time a toothache becomes obvious, the problem may have reached the nerve or weakened the tooth enough to require a crown, root canal, or extraction. X-rays help dentists see warning signs before the situation becomes urgent.

For example, a cavity between back teeth may not be visible in the mirror. Food may not get stuck there, and the tooth may not be sensitive yet. On an X-ray, that same cavity may be clear enough to treat with a filling before it spreads deeper. That is the value of preventive dentistry: find the problem early, explain it clearly, and treat it at the right time.

Dental X-rays for Kids and Teens

Parents often ask whether children should have dental X-rays. When they are needed, yes. Kids can get cavities between baby teeth, and those cavities are not always obvious during a visual exam. X-rays can also help track adult teeth, missing teeth, crowding, impacted teeth, and development concerns.

The recommendation should be individualized. A child with tight contacts, previous cavities, or high sugar exposure may need closer monitoring than a child with wide spacing and low cavity risk. If an X-ray is recommended, the reason should be clear and connected to your child's health, not routine for routine's sake.

Common Questions About Dental X-rays

Are dental X-rays safe?

Yes. Dental X-rays use low radiation exposure, and digital imaging helps keep that exposure limited while still providing useful diagnostic information.

How often do I need dental X-rays?

It depends on your dental history, symptoms, cavity risk, gum health, age, and existing dental work. Your dentist should recommend them based on your situation.

Why take X-rays if my teeth do not hurt?

Many dental problems begin before pain appears. X-rays can help find cavities, infection, bone loss, and issues under older dental work earlier.

Can children have dental X-rays?

Yes, when they are needed for diagnosis, cavity detection, growth monitoring, or development concerns.
