Is My Old Filling Leaking? Signs to Watch For in East Tennessee

Old fillings can weaken, chip, or lose their seal over time. Catching the problem early can protect the tooth before it turns into a bigger repair.

Published August 11, 2026  |  Elite Dental

A dental filling is meant to seal and strengthen a tooth after decay or damage has been removed. But fillings are not permanent. After years of chewing, clenching, temperature changes, and normal wear, the edge of a filling can begin to break down. When the seal opens, bacteria and fluid can work underneath the restoration. Patients often describe this as a leaking filling.

A leaking filling is not always obvious. Sometimes it hurts. Sometimes it only shows up on an exam or X-ray. The important thing is not to wait until the tooth becomes painful, because by then the problem may have grown from a simple filling replacement into a crown, root canal, or extraction conversation.

What Does a Leaking Filling Mean?

A filling leaks when the margin between the filling and the tooth no longer seals tightly. That tiny gap can collect plaque, food debris, and bacteria. Over time, new decay can form around or beneath the filling. The tooth may also flex slightly under chewing pressure, which can make a small gap worse.

This can happen with tooth-colored composite fillings or older silver fillings. Large fillings are especially vulnerable because there is less natural tooth structure left to support the bite.

Signs Your Filling May Be Failing

Common warning signs include sensitivity to cold, sweets, or pressure, food catching around the filling, floss that shreds in the same spot, a rough edge you can feel with your tongue, or a filling that looks darker around the border. Some patients notice a bad taste or smell when food gets trapped near the tooth.

Pain when biting can be a bigger warning sign. It may mean the filling is loose, the tooth is cracked, or decay has reached deeper than expected. If discomfort lingers after cold drinks or wakes you up at night, call promptly.

Small Symptoms Can Still Matter

A leaking filling may not cause constant pain. Mild sensitivity, a rough spot, or floss catching in one area can be enough reason to have the tooth checked. Early repairs are usually simpler, more comfortable, and less expensive than waiting for a toothache.

Why Old Fillings Start to Leak

Fillings live in a tough environment. They are exposed to chewing force, acidic drinks, sugar, brushing, grinding, and daily temperature swings from hot coffee to ice water. Over time, the bond can weaken or the edge can chip. If the filling is large, the remaining tooth can also crack around it.

Dry mouth increases the risk because saliva helps protect teeth from acid and bacteria. Patients who take medications that reduce saliva, sip sweet drinks throughout the day, or snack frequently may be more likely to develop decay around older fillings.

How Dentists Check a Suspected Leaking Filling

At Elite Dental, we look closely at the filling margins, check for soft or stained tooth structure, evaluate the bite, and use dental X-rays when needed. Not every dark line means decay, and not every sensitive tooth needs major work. The exam helps separate surface staining from a true failing restoration.

If the filling is small and the tooth is otherwise healthy, replacing the filling may be enough. If the filling is large, if the tooth has cracks, or if there is not enough strong enamel left, a crown may be the better long-term solution. If decay has reached the nerve, root canal treatment may be needed before the tooth can be rebuilt.

What You Should Do at Home

Keep the area clean and avoid chewing hard foods on that tooth until you are seen. Do not try to dig around the filling with a sharp tool. If floss catches, gently slide it out from the side instead of snapping it upward. Use fluoride toothpaste, drink water often, and limit frequent sugar or acidic drinks.

If part of a filling breaks off, save the piece if you can, but do not worry if you cannot find it. Call the office and describe what happened. The tooth needs to be evaluated even if it does not hurt.

Can a Leaking Filling Be Prevented?

You cannot make a filling last forever, but you can lower the risk of early failure. Brush twice daily, clean between the teeth, keep regular dental visits, and address grinding or clenching if your dentist sees signs of wear. A custom night guard can protect teeth and restorations from heavy nighttime forces.

Most importantly, do not ignore small changes. A filling that starts leaking is often fixable, especially when caught early. Waiting gives decay and cracks more time to spread.
