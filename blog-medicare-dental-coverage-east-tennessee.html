Does Medicare Cover Dental Care? What East Tennessee Seniors Should Know

Many people turning 65 assume Medicare will cover their cleanings, fillings, and dentures. Most of the time, it does not. Here is what is covered, what is not, and how to plan ahead.

Published November 10, 2026  |  Elite Dental

Every fall, during Medicare open enrollment, patients in our Dandridge and Jefferson City offices ask us the same question: "Will Medicare pay for my dental work?" It is a fair question. Medicare covers doctor visits, hospital stays, and prescriptions, so it seems natural that it would cover teeth too.

The honest answer is that Original Medicare covers very little dental care, and the dental benefits in many other plans are more limited than people expect. Understanding the details before you choose a plan can save you real money and help you keep your teeth for life.

The Short Version

Original Medicare (Parts A and B): does not cover routine dental care like exams, cleanings, fillings, extractions, or dentures.
The exceptions: limited dental care tied to certain covered medical treatments.
Medicare Advantage (Part C): many plans include some dental benefits, but annual limits and networks vary widely.
What to do: compare dental benefits carefully during open enrollment, October 15 through December 7, and ask your dentist what your mouth is likely to need.

What Original Medicare Does Not Cover

Original Medicare, which is Part A for hospital care and Part B for medical care, generally does not pay for the dental services most people need. That includes:

  • Routine exams and cleanings
  • X-rays taken for routine dental care
  • Fillings and crowns
  • Most tooth extractions
  • Root canals
  • Dentures, partials, and dental implants

The Limited Exceptions

Medicare may cover certain dental services when they are closely tied to a covered medical treatment. Examples include a dental exam before an organ transplant or heart valve replacement, or treating an infection before certain cancer treatments. Medicare has expanded these situations in recent years, but they remain narrow, and the rules about who qualifies are specific.

If you are facing a major medical procedure, ask your physician whether a dental clearance is part of your care plan. Our articles on dental care before cancer treatment and dental clearance before joint replacement explain why your medical team cares about your teeth.

Medicare Advantage Dental Benefits

Medicare Advantage plans, also called Part C, are offered by private insurance companies. Many of them include dental benefits, and that is often a major selling point in the ads you see this time of year. Before you choose a plan based on dental coverage, look closely at these details:

Annual maximum

This is the most the plan will pay for dental care in a year. Many plans set this limit at a level that covers preventive care and a filling or two, but not much more. A single crown, root canal, or set of dentures can use up the entire benefit.

What is actually covered

Some plans cover only preventive care, such as exams, cleanings, and X-rays. Others add fillings and extractions. Fewer cover crowns, dentures, or implants, and those that do often pay only a portion.

Networks

Many plans require you to see a dentist in their network, or they pay much less if you go out of network. If you already have a dentist you trust, check before you enroll.

Waiting periods and frequency limits

Some plans limit how often you can get cleanings or X-rays, or make you wait before major work is covered. Read the plan's Evidence of Coverage, not just the brochure.

Why Dental Care Matters More After 65

Skipping the dentist because insurance does not cover it is one of the most expensive decisions a retiree can make. Mouths change with age. Medications that cause dry mouth raise the risk of decay. Receding gums expose softer root surfaces, which leads to root cavities. Gum disease is linked to diabetes and heart health. Small problems caught at a checkup cost far less than the extractions, implants, or dentures they turn into when ignored.

Your Options If Medicare Will Not Pay

  • Compare Medicare Advantage dental benefits. During open enrollment, look at the annual maximum, covered services, and network, not just the monthly premium.

Questions to Ask Before You Enroll

Bring these questions to your insurance agent or plan representative:

  • What is the annual dental maximum?
  • Are crowns, root canals, dentures, and implants covered, and at what percentage?
  • Is my current dentist in the network?
  • Are there waiting periods for major treatment?
  • How many cleanings and exams are covered each year?

Common Questions

Does Medicare cover dental care?

Original Medicare, Parts A and B, does not cover most routine dental care such as exams, cleanings, fillings, extractions, or dentures. It may cover limited dental services that are closely tied to certain covered medical treatments.

Do Medicare Advantage plans cover dental?

Many Medicare Advantage plans include dental benefits, but coverage varies widely. Most have an annual maximum, and many cover preventive care more fully than major work like crowns, dentures, or implants. Check the plan details and network before enrolling.

When can I change my Medicare plan to get better dental coverage?

The annual Medicare open enrollment period runs from October 15 to December 7, with changes taking effect January 1. People already in a Medicare Advantage plan can also make one plan change from January 1 through March 31.
