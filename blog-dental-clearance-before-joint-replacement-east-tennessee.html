Dental Clearance Before Joint Replacement

A healthy mouth helps your surgical team reduce avoidable risk before a new hip or knee.

Published September 2, 2026  |  Elite Dental

You have a hip or knee replacement scheduled, and your orthopedic surgeon hands you a form for dental clearance. It can feel like one more appointment in an already busy stretch of preparation. Still, there is a practical reason your surgical team wants to know that your mouth is healthy before placing an artificial joint.

At Elite Dental, we help patients from Dandridge, Jefferson City, White Pine, Morristown, and surrounding East Tennessee communities complete preoperative dental evaluations. Here is what the process means, what we look for, and how to avoid a last-minute delay.

Why Your Surgeon May Request Dental Clearance

Your mouth naturally contains bacteria. Most are harmless when teeth and gums are healthy, but an untreated abscess or advanced gum infection can allow bacteria to enter the bloodstream. Because an artificial joint does not have the same natural defenses as living tissue, orthopedic teams take active infection seriously.

Dental clearance does not promise that every tooth will remain problem-free forever. It tells the surgical team that we examined you and found no obvious active dental infection requiring attention before surgery, or that any identified problem has been treated. Your surgeon sets the final requirements and decides whether you are ready to proceed.

What Happens at the Appointment

We begin by reviewing your health history, medications, planned procedure, and the form or instructions from your surgeon. Tell us your surgery date and bring any paperwork the orthopedic office provided. Also bring an updated medication list, including blood thinners and medicines prescribed specifically for surgery.

Next, we examine your teeth, gums, and existing dental work. We look for swelling, drainage, deep decay, broken teeth, significant gum infection, and other signs that could indicate an active problem. X-rays may be recommended based on your examination, recent dental records, symptoms, and the surgeon's request. This is a focused health evaluation, not an automatic requirement for extensive elective treatment.

Do Not Wait Until the Week Before Surgery

Schedule dental clearance as soon as your surgeon asks for it. If a tooth needs treatment or removal, your mouth may need time to heal before the orthopedic procedure. Starting several weeks ahead gives your dentist and surgeon time to coordinate without unnecessary pressure.

What If We Find a Problem?

The next step depends on the diagnosis and your surgeon's protocol. A cavity with no sign of infection may be handled differently from an abscess, severe periodontal infection, or tooth that cannot be restored. We will explain what we see, what treatment is appropriate, and how quickly it should be completed.

Communication matters. Your dentist can document findings and completed care, but your orthopedic surgeon makes the final decision about timing. If treatment is needed, the offices may coordinate to determine an appropriate healing period. Never stop a blood thinner, antibiotic, or other prescription on your own. Medication changes must come from the clinician who prescribed it or from your surgical team.

Will I Need Antibiotics?

Antibiotics are not a substitute for treating an active dental infection. Whether you need antibiotic premedication for dental care before or after joint replacement depends on your medical history, procedure, and the recommendations of your dentist and orthopedic surgeon. Guidance is individualized, especially for patients with prior joint complications, immune system concerns, or other significant medical conditions.

If your surgeon has given you written antibiotic instructions, bring them with you. We can review those instructions and contact the orthopedic office when clarification is necessary.

How to Prepare Before Your Visit

Continue brushing twice daily and cleaning between your teeth. Write down any recent toothache, swelling, gum bleeding, bad taste, loose tooth, or pain when chewing, even if it has improved. Symptoms that come and go can still help us locate a problem. If another dentist has taken recent X-rays, ask whether those records can be transferred before your appointment.

After joint replacement, follow your surgeon's instructions about when routine or elective dental care can resume. If you develop urgent dental pain or swelling, contact a dentist promptly and tell both offices about your recent surgery.

Common Questions

Why do I need dental clearance before joint replacement?

Your surgical team wants active dental infections identified and treated before an artificial joint is placed.

How early should I schedule?

Call as soon as the request is made, ideally several weeks before surgery. Extra time matters if treatment and healing are necessary.

Does every cavity delay joint surgery?

Not necessarily. The diagnosis, infection risk, surgeon's requirements, and treatment timing all matter. Your dentist and orthopedic team should make that decision together.

Can Elite Dental complete my surgeon's form?

We can perform the requested dental evaluation and provide documentation based on our findings. Bring the form and your surgeon's instructions to the appointment.

Need Dental Clearance?

Call early so we have time to examine your mouth, address any concerns, and return the requested documentation.
