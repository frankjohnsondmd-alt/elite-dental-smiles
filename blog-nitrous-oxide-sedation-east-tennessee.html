Nitrous Oxide Sedation · East Tennessee

Nitrous Oxide (Laughing Gas) in East Tennessee:
What It Feels Like & Who It Helps

The mildest form of dental sedation, available at Elite Dental in Dandridge and Jefferson City, TN.

By Dr. George F. Johnson III, DMD · Elite Dental · October 2026

Most people who are nervous about the dentist do not need deep sedation. They need the volume turned down. Nitrous oxide in East Tennessee, better known as laughing gas, does exactly that: it takes a visit that feels like something to dread and turns it into something closer to ordinary.

It is the most commonly used sedation method in dentistry, and it is also the most misunderstood. Patients regularly tell me they assumed it would knock them out, or that they would giggle uncontrollably, or that they would need someone to drive them home. None of that is true. Here is what nitrous oxide actually does.

What Nitrous Oxide Actually Is

Nitrous oxide is a colorless gas with a faintly sweet smell. In the dental setting it is never given on its own. It is blended with oxygen and delivered through a small, soft mask that rests over your nose. You breathe normally. Your mouth stays free for treatment.

The effect begins within about three to five minutes. Most patients describe a warm, heavy, slightly floaty sensation, often starting in the hands and feet. Sounds soften. Time compresses. The appointment that would have felt like an hour of white knuckles feels like a few minutes of sitting still.

Critically, the concentration is adjustable throughout the visit. If you feel too light, we increase it. If you feel too floaty, we dial it back and you return to baseline within a couple of minutes. Very few sedation options give you that kind of real time control.

Nitrous oxide is the only sedation option that can be increased, decreased, and completely reversed inside the same appointment.

What It Is Not

Nitrous oxide is classified as minimal sedation. You remain fully awake and conversational. You will remember your appointment. It does not replace local anesthetic, so you still get numbed for anything that requires it. And despite the nickname, uncontrollable laughter is not what happens at dental concentrations. The nickname is a holdover from 1800s stage demonstrations, not a description of modern practice.

Who Nitrous Oxide Helps Most

In our Dandridge and Jefferson City offices, nitrous oxide tends to be the right answer for five groups of patients:

  • Mild to moderate dental anxiety. Not the patient who has avoided care for fifteen years, but the one who sleeps poorly the night before a cleaning. Nitrous handles this well.
  • A strong gag reflex. Nitrous reduces the gag response enough to make impressions, x-rays, and back teeth work genuinely tolerable.
  • Children. It is the workhorse of pediatric dentistry. A nervous child can get a filling done calmly and go back to school the same afternoon.
  • Patients who cannot arrange a driver. Oral and IV sedation require someone to take you home. Nitrous usually does not.
  • Long or tedious appointments. Even patients who are not anxious often request it simply because it makes a two hour visit pass faster.

It is a poor fit for a few situations. Patients with severe dental phobia, those needing extensive surgery in one visit, and patients who want little or no memory of the procedure are usually better served by oral sedation or IV sedation. Patients who cannot breathe comfortably through the nose will not get a reliable result from it at all.

What the Appointment Looks Like

We start with your health history. Nitrous oxide interacts with a short list of medical conditions, so we review respiratory issues, nasal congestion, vitamin B12 status, recent eye surgery, and pregnancy before recommending it.

At the appointment, the nasal hood goes on and you breathe normally for a few minutes while we bring the concentration up to a comfortable level. Treatment proceeds as usual. We monitor you throughout and adjust as needed.

When the procedure is finished, we switch you to one hundred percent oxygen for roughly five minutes. This clears the nitrous oxide from your system and reverses the effect almost completely. Most patients feel entirely normal before they stand up. That five minutes of oxygen is the entire recovery period, which is why nitrous oxide is the only sedation option where going straight back to work is routine.

How It Compares to Oral and IV Sedation

Sedation is not a single thing. It is a spectrum, and matching the method to the patient matters more than picking the strongest option available.

Nitrous oxide is minimal sedation. Onset in minutes, fully adjustable, recovery in minutes, no driver needed, lowest cost. Best for mild to moderate anxiety and gag reflex.

Oral sedation is a pill taken before the appointment. It reaches a deeper level than nitrous, but onset and depth vary from person to person and cannot be adjusted once taken. You will need a driver and most of the day free.

IV sedation delivers medication directly to the bloodstream for precise, immediately adjustable moderate sedation. It is the right answer for severe phobia, complex implant cases, and full mouth work. It requires a state issued permit, which relatively few dentists in East Tennessee hold. Dr. Johnson does, which means all three levels are available under one roof.

That matters more than it sounds. A practice that only offers nitrous will push you toward nitrous whether or not it fits. A practice that only offers IV sedation has every reason to recommend the most involved option. Having the full range available means the recommendation can simply be the honest one.

Frequently Asked Questions About Nitrous Oxide

Does nitrous oxide actually make you laugh? +

Rarely. The nickname 'laughing gas' comes from 19th century demonstrations where people inhaled very high concentrations for entertainment. At the carefully controlled levels used in dentistry, nitrous oxide produces a calm, warm, slightly floaty feeling rather than giggling. Most patients describe it as pleasantly detached, as if the appointment is happening a short distance away from them.

Will I be asleep or unconscious with laughing gas? +

No. Nitrous oxide is minimal sedation. You stay fully awake, you can talk with Dr. Johnson and the team, and you will remember the appointment. The goal is to take the edge off anxiety and make time pass more easily, not to put you under. If you need a deeper level of relaxation or want little memory of the visit, oral sedation or IV sedation may be a better fit.

Can I drive myself home after nitrous oxide? +

In most cases yes, and that is one of the biggest advantages. Nitrous oxide leaves your system within minutes. At the end of your appointment we have you breathe pure oxygen for roughly five minutes, which clears the gas and reverses the effects. Most patients feel completely normal before they leave the chair and go straight back to work or school. We will still confirm you feel steady before you drive.

Is nitrous oxide safe for children? +

Nitrous oxide is widely used in pediatric dentistry and has a long record of safe use when a child is properly screened and monitored. It is often the ideal option for a nervous child who needs a filling or a sealant, because it reduces anxiety without the recovery time of deeper sedation. Every child is evaluated beforehand, and we review health history, medications, and whether the child can breathe comfortably through the nose.

Who should not use nitrous oxide? +

Nitrous oxide is not appropriate for everyone. It may be avoided in patients with certain respiratory conditions, a severely blocked nose or sinus congestion, some vitamin B12 deficiencies, certain recent eye surgeries, and in the first trimester of pregnancy. Patients who cannot breathe through the nose will not get a reliable effect. We review your full health history before recommending it.

How much does nitrous oxide cost compared to other sedation? +

Nitrous oxide is by far the least expensive sedation option because it requires no IV, no extended monitoring, and no recovery period. Our Dandridge location is fee for service, so we do not participate with insurance networks though we can provide documentation for potential reimbursement. Our Jefferson City location works with some insurance plans. Call either office and our team can walk you through the specifics for your situation.
