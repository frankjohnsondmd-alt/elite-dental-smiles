Tooth Pain After a Root Canal: What Is Normal and When to Call

Some soreness is part of healing. Here is how long it usually lasts, how to manage it, and the signs that mean you should call.

Published October 9, 2026  |  Elite Dental

You finally got the root canal done, and now the tooth is sore. That is the moment a lot of patients call us worried that something went wrong. Most of the time, nothing did. Some tenderness after a root canal is a normal part of healing. But there are a few signs that do mean you should pick up the phone, and it helps to know the difference.

At Elite Dental, we perform root canal treatment for patients from Dandridge, Jefferson City, White Pine, Morristown, and across East Tennessee. Here is what to expect in the days after, and what is worth a call.

Why a Tooth Can Be Sore After the Nerve Is Gone

A root canal removes the infected or inflamed nerve from inside the tooth, so the tooth itself can no longer feel hot or cold. What you feel afterward usually comes from the tissue around the root. The tooth sits in a small cushion of ligament fibers, and that cushion was often already irritated by the infection before treatment. The cleaning and shaping of the canals can stir it up a little more.

The result is a tooth that feels bruised, especially when you bite down or tap on it. Your jaw may also be stiff from holding your mouth open, and the spot where you were numbed can be tender for a day or two.

What Normal Healing Looks Like

For most patients, the pattern looks like this:

  • First night: a dull ache as the numbness wears off.
  • Days one to three: tenderness when chewing on that side, mild soreness in the gum.
  • Days three to seven: steady improvement, with each day a little better than the last.
  • One to two weeks: the tooth feels close to normal, though a firm bite may still feel slightly different.

The key word is improvement. Normal soreness peaks early and fades. Pain that climbs instead of falling is the thing to watch.

How to Manage Soreness at Home

For most healthy adults, an over-the-counter anti-inflammatory such as ibuprofen works very well for this kind of soreness because the problem is inflammation. Some patients do best alternating ibuprofen with acetaminophen. Follow the dosing on the label or the instructions we give you, and check with your physician if you have stomach, kidney, or heart issues or take blood thinners.

A few other simple things help:

  • Chew on the other side until the tooth settles down.
  • Stick with softer foods for the first few days.
  • Avoid very hard or sticky foods that could crack the tooth or pull out the temporary filling.
  • A warm salt water rinse can soothe sore gum tissue.
  • Keep brushing and flossing gently around the tooth.

Call Us If You Notice Any of These

Pain that gets worse after the third day instead of better. Swelling in your gum, cheek, or jaw, or swelling that is spreading. A fever. A bad taste or drainage near the tooth. Pain that over-the-counter medicine does not touch. A temporary filling that falls out or a tooth that feels like it cracked. If swelling affects your breathing or swallowing, go to the emergency room right away.

The Bite Problem That Is Easy to Fix

One of the most common reasons a tooth stays sore after a root canal is simple: the temporary filling or new crown sits slightly high. Every time you close, that one tooth hits first and presses on a ligament that is already inflamed. It can feel like the root canal failed when really the bite just needs a small adjustment.

If your tooth hurts mostly when your teeth come together, and it feels like it hits before the others, call us. Adjusting the bite takes a few minutes and often brings relief within a day or two. We see the same thing with fillings, which we cover in our article on a tooth that feels high after a filling.

A Flare-Up

A small percentage of patients have what dentists call a flare-up, usually within the first couple of days. The tooth becomes much more painful than expected, and sometimes swelling develops. This happens when the body reacts strongly to bacteria or debris that were stirred up around the root tip. It is not a sign that you did anything wrong, and it is usually very treatable. We may prescribe an antibiotic if there is swelling, adjust the bite, or open and clean the tooth again. The sooner we hear from you, the sooner it calms down.

When Pain Returns Weeks or Years Later

Pain that shows up after the tooth had been comfortable is a different situation. Possible causes include a canal that was hard to find or reach, new decay that let bacteria back in, a leaking temporary filling, or a crack in the root. A tooth that was never crowned is especially at risk of cracking.

Many of these teeth can still be saved. Depending on what we find, options include root canal retreatment, a referral to a specialist for a minor surgery at the root tip, or in some cases removing the tooth and replacing it with an implant. A 3D scan often helps us see what regular X-rays cannot.

Do Not Skip the Crown

A root canal saves the tooth, but it does not make it stronger. Treated back teeth become more brittle over time, and a crack can end the tooth for good. That is why molars and premolars almost always need a crown after a root canal. Plan to have it placed within a few weeks, and protect the temporary in the meantime by following our temporary crown care tips. Delaying the crown is one of the most common reasons we see a root canal tooth lost that did not need to be.

Common Questions

How long should a tooth hurt after a root canal?

Most patients feel tenderness for two to five days, mostly when biting. It should get a little better each day. Soreness that is getting worse after three days, or that lasts beyond two weeks, should be checked.

Is throbbing pain normal after a root canal?

Mild aching the first night is common as the numbness wears off. Strong throbbing pain that keeps you awake or is not helped by over-the-counter pain relievers is not typical and you should call your dentist.

Why does my tooth hurt when I bite after a root canal?

The ligament around the root is often inflamed for a few days after treatment. If the temporary filling sits slightly high, every bite presses on that sore ligament. A quick bite adjustment usually brings fast relief.

Can a root canal tooth still feel hot and cold?

A tooth with a completed root canal should not react to hot or cold because the nerve has been removed. If you still feel temperature sensitivity, a neighboring tooth may be the source, or a canal may need another look.

Do I really need a crown after a root canal?

Back teeth almost always need a crown. A treated tooth is more brittle, and a crack can end the tooth. Getting the crown placed within a few weeks protects the investment you made in saving the tooth.
