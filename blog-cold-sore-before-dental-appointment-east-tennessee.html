Cold Sore Before a Dental Appointment? What to Do

That familiar tingle shows up two days before your cleaning. Here is why it matters, when to call us, and how to keep cold sores from crashing your next visit.

Published October 15, 2026  |  Elite Dental

Cold sores have a way of appearing at the worst possible time. A wedding, a big presentation, or the morning of a dental appointment you booked six months ago. If you just felt that tingle, the short answer is simple: call the office before you come in. In most cases we will move a routine visit back a week or so.

What a Cold Sore Actually Is

Cold sores, also called fever blisters, are caused by the herpes simplex virus, usually type 1. Most adults carry it. After the first infection it stays dormant in the nerves and can flare up later, usually at the edge of the lip.

An outbreak tends to follow a predictable pattern:

  • Tingling or burning. A day or two before anything is visible, the spot may itch, burn, or feel tight.
  • Blisters. Small fluid-filled bumps form, often in a cluster.
  • Weeping and crusting. The blisters break open, ooze, and then form a scab.
  • Healing. The scab falls off and the skin heals, typically within 7 to 10 days total.

The sore is most contagious from the first tingle until it has fully crusted over, and the virus can spread through direct contact during that window.

Why We Often Reschedule Routine Visits

Postponing a cleaning over a small blister can feel like overkill, but there are real reasons behind it, and most of them are about you.

  • It slows your healing. Dental work means stretching your lips and holding your mouth open for a while. That pulls on the sore, can crack it open, and often makes it last longer and hurt more.
  • It can spread the virus. Moving an open sore around can carry the virus to other spots on your lips, the corners of your mouth, or your fingers.
  • It protects the next person. Our team uses gloves, masks, and full infection control on every patient, but keeping an active outbreak out of a close-contact setting is still the safest call.

Pain or an Emergency? Still Call Us

A cold sore is not a reason to sit at home with a toothache, a swollen face, a broken tooth, or an infection. If you have a dental emergency, call either office right away and let us know about the cold sore when you do. We will decide together what needs to happen now and what can wait until your lip heals. See our emergency dentistry page for more.

When It Is Fine to Come In

Once the blister has crusted over completely, the scab is dry, and nothing is open or weeping, routine dental work is usually fine. For most people that is about a week to ten days after the first tingle. If you are not sure, snap a quick look in the mirror and describe it to the front desk. We would much rather answer a phone call than have you guess.

How to Help a Cold Sore Heal Faster

Most cold sores heal on their own, but a few simple steps can make the outbreak shorter and less painful:

  • Start early. Over-the-counter docosanol cream works best when you apply it at the first tingle, not after the blister forms.
  • Ask about antivirals. If you get frequent or severe outbreaks, your physician can prescribe an antiviral medication that shortens outbreaks when started early.
  • Hands off. Do not pick, pop, or squeeze the blister. Wash your hands after touching it, and keep it away from your eyes.
  • Keep it clean and protected. A plain lip balm on the surrounding skin helps prevent cracking. Use a separate tube or a cotton swab so you do not reinfect yourself later.
  • Do not share. Skip kissing, shared drinks, utensils, and towels until it heals.

Can Dental Work Trigger a Cold Sore?

For people who carry the virus, yes, it can. Common triggers include lip stretching and irritation during longer procedures, dry and chapped lips, sun exposure, illness, and stress. A long dental visit can check several of those boxes at once.

If you know you are prone to cold sores, let us know when you book. A few simple things can help:

  • Keep your lips well moisturized for several days before your appointment.
  • Use a lip balm with SPF if you have been outdoors, since sun is a common trigger, especially for anyone spending weekends on Douglas Lake or Cherokee Lake.
  • Ask your physician whether a preventive antiviral before longer procedures, like crowns or implant surgery, is right for you.
  • Tell your hygienist or dentist. We can apply lip lubricant and give your lips frequent breaks during the visit.

If dental anxiety tends to stress you out before appointments, that stress itself can contribute to outbreaks. Our sedation dentistry options can make longer visits far more relaxed.

Cold Sore or Canker Sore?

People mix these up all the time, but they are different. Cold sores usually show up on the outside of the lip and are caused by a virus. Canker sores appear inside the mouth, on the cheeks, gums, or tongue, and are not contagious. A canker sore usually does not require rescheduling. Learn more in our guide to canker sores.

When to See a Doctor

Call your physician if a sore lasts longer than two weeks, keeps coming back very often, spreads near your eyes, comes with a high fever, or if you have a weakened immune system. Any sore in or around the mouth that does not heal should be looked at, which is one reason an oral cancer screening is part of your regular checkups with us.

Common Questions

Should I cancel my dental appointment if I have a cold sore?

Call the office first. For routine cleanings and cosmetic work we usually reschedule until the sore has fully crusted and healed, because stretching the lips can spread the virus and slow healing. Dental pain or emergencies are still treated.

How long should I wait after a cold sore to see the dentist?

Most cold sores heal in about 7 to 10 days. Once the blister has crusted over completely and the skin is no longer weeping or open, routine dental care is usually fine.

Can dental work trigger a cold sore?

Yes. Stretching the lips, dry lips, and stress can all trigger an outbreak in people who carry the virus. If you get cold sores often, ask your physician whether an antiviral before a long dental visit makes sense for you.

Need to Move Your Appointment?

Just give us a call. We will find a time that works once your lip has healed, or get you in right away if you are dealing with dental pain.
