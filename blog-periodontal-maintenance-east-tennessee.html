Periodontal Maintenance in East Tennessee: Why It Matters After a Deep Cleaning

Once gum disease has been treated, the goal changes from catching up to staying stable.

Published July 30, 2026  |  Elite Dental

If you have had a deep cleaning, also called scaling and root planing, your dentist or hygienist may recommend periodontal maintenance instead of a regular six month cleaning. That can sound confusing at first. The appointment may look similar from the chair, but the reason for it is different.

A regular cleaning is preventive care for patients with generally healthy gums. Periodontal maintenance is ongoing therapy for patients who have shown signs of gum disease, bone loss, deeper pockets, or recurring inflammation. It is designed to keep gum disease from gaining ground again.

Why Gum Disease Needs Follow-Up Care

Gum disease is not usually a one-time problem. Once bacteria have caused pocketing around the teeth or bone loss beneath the gums, those areas can stay more vulnerable. Even after a successful deep cleaning, plaque and tartar can return below the gumline faster than most people realize.

The problem is not just dirty teeth. Gum pockets can create sheltered spaces where bacteria are harder to reach with brushing and flossing. If inflammation comes back, the gums may bleed, pockets can deepen, and the supporting bone around the teeth can continue to break down.

How Often Is Periodontal Maintenance Needed?

Many patients do best with periodontal maintenance every three to four months. That schedule is not meant to punish anyone. It is based on how quickly bacteria can reorganize under the gumline and restart inflammation. For some patients, waiting six months is simply too long.

Your exact interval depends on several factors: pocket depths, bleeding points, tartar buildup, smoking or vaping, diabetes control, dry mouth, medications, clenching, and how easy it is to clean between your teeth at home. If the gums become more stable over time, the schedule may change.

Stability Is the Win

Periodontal maintenance is not about chasing a perfect report card. It is about keeping the gums quiet, protecting bone support, and helping teeth last longer.

What Happens During a Maintenance Visit?

A periodontal maintenance visit usually includes a gum health check, removal of plaque and tartar above and below the gumline, polishing, and focused home care coaching. Your hygienist may measure gum pockets periodically to see whether areas are improving, staying stable, or getting worse.

If one area keeps bleeding or trapping buildup, the plan can be adjusted before it turns into a larger problem. Sometimes that means more detailed cleaning in a specific area. Sometimes it means discussing bite forces, dry mouth, an electric toothbrush, water flossing, or a different way to clean tight spaces.

Can You Go Back to Regular Cleanings?

Some patients can return to regular cleanings after their gums are stable for a sustained period. Others stay healthier with periodontal maintenance long term. The decision should be based on what your gums are doing, not just what insurance calls the appointment.

It is also important to know that skipping maintenance can allow inflammation to return quietly. Gum disease often does not hurt until it is advanced. By the time teeth feel loose or chewing becomes uncomfortable, more support may already be gone.

Common Questions About Periodontal Maintenance

Is periodontal maintenance the same as a regular cleaning?

No. A regular cleaning is preventive. Periodontal maintenance is ongoing care after gum disease has been diagnosed or treated.

Will it hurt?

Most visits are comfortable, especially when maintenance is done on schedule. If a spot is tender, tell your hygienist so the visit can be adjusted.

Why does insurance handle it differently?

Insurance plans often separate preventive cleanings from periodontal therapy. Coverage depends on the plan, but the clinical reason is gum stability.
