Signs of a Tooth Abscess and When to Seek Care

A dental infection rarely resolves on its own. Knowing the warning signs can help you get treatment before it becomes more serious.

Published August 26, 2026  |  Elite Dental

A tooth abscess is a pocket of infection caused by bacteria. It can form near the root of a tooth or in the gum tissue beside it. Some abscesses begin with severe pain, while others develop quietly and are first noticed as swelling, a bad taste, or a small bump on the gum.

Whatever the symptoms, a suspected dental abscess deserves prompt attention. The infection can damage the tooth and surrounding bone, and it can spread beyond the mouth. Pain medicine may make you feel better temporarily, but it does not remove the source of the infection.

Common Signs of a Dental Abscess

Throbbing tooth pain is a familiar warning sign. The discomfort may be constant, worsen when you lie down, or spread toward the jaw, ear, or neck. The tooth may hurt when you bite or feel unusually sensitive to hot and cold temperatures.

Other symptoms include swelling in the gum, face, or jaw; a tender lump under the jaw; red or puffy gums; fever; and a persistent bad taste or odor. A pimple-like bump on the gum may release fluid and briefly reduce pressure. That drainage does not mean the infection is gone.

When to Get Emergency Help

Go to an emergency department or call 911 if swelling makes it hard to breathe or swallow, spreads toward the eye or neck, or is accompanied by confusion or severe illness. These symptoms can signal that the infection is spreading and should not wait for a routine dental appointment.

Why Tooth Abscesses Develop

Deep tooth decay is a common cause. Once bacteria reach the soft pulp inside a tooth, the tissue can become infected and die. Cracks, fractures, leaking fillings, and trauma can also give bacteria a path into the tooth. Gum disease can create a separate type of abscess when bacteria collect in a deep pocket beside a tooth.

An abscess can sometimes exist without much pain if pressure drains through the gum or if the nerve inside the tooth has died. That is why a darkened tooth, recurring gum bump, unexplained bad taste, or localized swelling still needs an examination even when it is not painful.

What You Can Do Until Your Appointment

Call your dentist as soon as possible and describe your pain, swelling, fever, and how long the symptoms have been present. While waiting, rinse gently with warm salt water and keep the area clean with careful brushing. Choose soft foods, chew on the other side, and avoid very hot, cold, or sugary items if they trigger pain.

Over-the-counter pain relievers may help when taken according to the label and your medical provider's guidance. Do not place aspirin directly against the tooth or gum because it can burn the tissue. Do not squeeze or puncture a swollen area, and never take leftover antibiotics prescribed for someone else or for a previous illness.

How a Dentist Treats an Abscess

Treatment depends on where the infection started and whether the tooth can be predictably saved. The dentist may examine the area, take X-rays, and test the tooth. Root canal treatment can remove infected tissue from inside a restorable tooth. A gum abscess may need drainage and periodontal treatment. If the tooth is too damaged to save, extraction may be the safest option.

Antibiotics are useful in certain cases, especially when there is fever, spreading swelling, or other evidence that the infection is affecting the body. However, antibiotics alone usually do not correct the infected tooth. Definitive dental treatment is needed to eliminate the source and reduce the chance that the infection returns.

Do Not Wait for the Pain to Become Severe

Dental infections are generally easier to treat before swelling spreads or the tooth breaks down further. Early care may also provide more options for saving the tooth. If symptoms improve suddenly, the nerve may have died or the abscess may have drained, but the underlying problem can still remain active.
