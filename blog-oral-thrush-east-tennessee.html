Oral Thrush: Signs, Causes, and When to See a Dentist

What those white patches may mean and how to keep the problem from returning.

Published September 9, 2026  |  Elite Dental

A white coating on your tongue does not always mean oral thrush. It may come from dehydration, food debris, or ordinary buildup. But creamy white patches that spread to the cheeks, gums, roof of the mouth, or throat can be a sign of a yeast infection called oral candidiasis.

Oral thrush is usually manageable, but it should not be diagnosed from appearance alone. Other oral conditions can look similar, and recurring thrush may point to an underlying health issue that needs attention. A dental evaluation can help identify the cause and guide the right treatment.

What Does Oral Thrush Look and Feel Like?

Thrush often appears as raised, creamy white patches that resemble cottage cheese. The patches may wipe away, leaving red, tender tissue that can bleed slightly. Some people have redness without obvious white patches, cracks at the corners of the mouth, a cottony sensation, or a reduced sense of taste.

Eating spicy, salty, or acidic foods may sting. In more advanced cases, swallowing can become uncomfortable. Babies, older adults, denture wearers, and people with weakened immune systems may be more likely to develop symptoms, though thrush can affect anyone when the normal balance of microorganisms in the mouth changes.

What Causes Oral Thrush?

Candida yeast normally lives in the mouth in small amounts. Helpful bacteria and the immune system usually keep it controlled. Thrush develops when that balance is disrupted. A recent course of antibiotics is a common trigger because antibiotics can reduce the bacteria that normally limit yeast growth.

Other risk factors include dry mouth, diabetes that is not well controlled, immune-suppressing conditions or medications, chemotherapy, and inhaled corticosteroids used for asthma or lung disease. Smoking and poor nutrition may also contribute.

Dentures create another place for yeast to collect, especially when they are worn overnight or not cleaned thoroughly. A denture that rubs or fits poorly can leave irritated tissue more vulnerable. People who use a steroid inhaler can reduce risk by rinsing with water and spitting after each dose, while following their prescriber's instructions.

When Symptoms Need Prompt Attention

Contact a healthcare professional promptly if white patches come with trouble swallowing, fever, dehydration, significant pain, or a weakened immune system. A mouth lesion that does not improve within two weeks also needs an examination.

How Is Oral Thrush Treated?

Treatment depends on the diagnosis, severity, and reason the infection developed. A dentist or physician may prescribe an antifungal rinse, lozenge, or tablet. Use the medication exactly as directed and complete the prescribed course, even if symptoms begin improving sooner.

Treatment also needs to address the trigger. That might mean improving denture hygiene, managing dry mouth, reviewing recent medications with your medical provider, or checking blood sugar. Never stop a prescribed medicine on your own. If thrush keeps returning, your dentist may recommend coordination with your physician to look for an underlying cause.

Can You Prevent Thrush From Returning?

Brush twice daily with fluoride toothpaste and clean between your teeth every day. Clean your tongue gently, but do not scrape sore tissue aggressively. Keep removable dentures clean, remove them at night unless your dentist specifically says otherwise, and clean the tissues under them. Your dentist can show you which cleaning products are safe for your denture material.

Drink water regularly and ask about dry-mouth strategies if your mouth often feels sticky. If you have diabetes, keeping blood sugar within the range recommended by your medical team can reduce infection risk. Replace toothbrushes as advised during treatment, avoid sharing oral-care items, and keep regular dental visits so irritation or denture problems are found early.

Common Questions About Oral Thrush

Is every white tongue thrush?

No. Dehydration, buildup, irritation, and several oral conditions can create a white appearance. An exam is the safest way to distinguish them.

Is oral thrush contagious?

It is not usually considered contagious in healthy adults. Candida can pass between people, but symptoms typically develop when the body's normal defenses are disrupted.

Will mouthwash cure oral thrush?

Ordinary mouthwash is not a substitute for diagnosis or antifungal treatment. Some alcohol-containing rinses may make a dry or sore mouth feel worse.

Can dentures cause thrush?

Dentures do not directly cause it, but yeast can collect on them. Daily cleaning, nighttime removal, and a proper fit help lower the risk.
